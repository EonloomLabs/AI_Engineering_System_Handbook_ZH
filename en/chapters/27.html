Prerequisites for this chapter

Fluency, confidence, an internal explanation and external correctness are not the same quantity. An image representation, too, has to enter numeric computation first; research on interpretability helps observe part of the mechanism, and cannot recover every reason for a decision on its own.

Each question in this chapter lists the groundwork it needs. You can take the matching Foundations lesson first, or use the self-check to confirm how well you have it.

A quick check before the deep reading: tell "the model says it is 90% sure" apart from "of past answers of this kind given 90% confidence, 90% really were right"; only the second involves evidence of calibration.

Going deep into a system also means knowing what the system cannot prove. A fluent model output is not factual correctness; a self-reported confidence is not calibration; seeing a screenshot is not understanding the business state; finding a correlated activation is not having explained every reason.

Q101｜Why does an LLM "state an error confidently"? How do you tell confidence, calibration and selective answering apart?

What to know first for this question A confidence score is not a calibrated rate of correctness; answering from evidence, being allowed to abstain, and verifying afterwards together reduce the risk of invention.
Lesson 44 · Probability, exponentials and softmaxLesson 54 · The mathematics needed before post-trainingLesson 05 · The denominator of an evaluation metricLesson 31 · Do not be fooled by a few percentLesson 64 · Multimodality and trustworthiness

Core answer

What a generative model picks is an output that is likely under the given context and its trained behavior, not a lookup in a ground-truth database before every fact. An error may come from missing parametric knowledge, wrong evidence, a retrieval omission, a slip in reasoning, or a stylistic preference that does not fit. The relation between a confident tone and the real rate of correctness needs empirical calibration; the two are not automatically the same.[TRUTHQA][CALIBRATION]

Begin by separating the probability objects

Token probability describes the distribution of the "next token"; it is not directly the probability that the whole answer is right. A correct answer can be worded in many ways; a wrong answer may equally consist of a string of high-probability words. Taking the average token probability as factual confidence mixes in how common the sentence pattern is, the length and the language style.

A model's self-reported "90% sure" is another output, and has to be checked against tasks whose ground truth is known. In the binary case the Brier score is平均(pᵢ−yᵢ)². For example, with three predicted probabilities 0.9, 0.6 and 0.2 and true outcomes 1, 0 and 0, the score is (0.01+0.36+0.04)/3≈0.1367. This score reflects the quality of the probabilities taken together; it does not mean that any single question has been proved.

A reliability diagram sorts the predictions into bins and compares each bin's average confidence with its actual rate of correctness. ECE weights the absolute difference between the two by each bin's share, but the result depends on the binning, the sample size and the task distribution; a model with a low overall ECE may still be badly miscalibrated on a rare, high-risk task.

Selective answering lets the system abstain or ask for more when the evidence is not enough. Coverage and the error rate on what was answered have to be read together: answering only the easiest 10% of questions makes errors rare without showing that the system is strong overall. In engineering, report both "how much was answered" and "how well it was answered" rather than accuracy alone.

Whether "I am 80% sure" is reliable has to be calibrated against a set of actual results

Conditions for this example: an independent offline evaluation: 100 answers for which the system claims a correctness probability of about 0.8 are picked out, and manual checking against a fixed standard confirms that only 60 are correct. This is not a measurement of any model.

How to read the changes in the figure

Calibration needs statistics over many questions and cannot rest on a single answer. Neither confident wording nor the vocabulary softmax can be taken directly as confidence in a fact.

Raising the abstention threshold usually reduces coverage, so risk and coverage have to be reported together rather than showing only the high accuracy of the answers that remain.

Calibration can change under a different task distribution, and production feedback has to be monitored afresh.

Do not conflate: taking a judgment generated by the same model as the only truth lets a "grade yourself, prove yourself" loop hide the errors.

Follow-up: once a real source has been retrieved, will there be no more hallucination?

It may still misread a condition, cite a paragraph that does not support the claim, mix different versions, or add detail beyond the evidence. RAG reduces some of the missing knowledge, but introduces new failure points in retrieval and in integrating evidence. What is needed is a support check at the level of each claim, not just a check of whether the answer carries a link.

Follow-up: can having the model answer several times and taking a majority vote solve it?

On some decidable tasks it may improve things, but repeated errors are often correlated: the same model and the same context share a misunderstanding. Agreement by majority is not proof of fact. Calling an independent calculator, a compiler or the original data usually changes the evidence conditions more than asking the same question again.

Follow-up: can a low probability simply be set as the abstention threshold?

The threshold has to be calibrated on representative data, with the cost of an error, the coverage target and distribution shift all taken into account. A 0.8 from one benchmark cannot be carried over to every business. After going live, watch new categories, languages and levels of complexity, and recalibrate where necessary.

Engineering deep dive · E53 · continues Q105

If only the questions you are sure of are answered, why might the rise in accuracy be nothing but a fall in coverage?

Prerequisite: Lesson 44 · Lesson 05 · Lesson 31. Reading order: confirm the runtime contract first, then look at the counterexamples and the fix, and finally test the limits.

Three kinds of number cannot stand in for one another

The next-token probability, the model's verbally stated confidence score and the actual rate of correctness on the complete task are not the same random variable. To use one of these scores to decide whether to answer, look at how that score relates to the real outcome on the target task. The calibration documentation distinguishes reliability, discrimination and overall error, and requires suitable calibration data.[CAL]

A fixed set of ten questions to show selection bias

The teaching system answers 5 of ten questions correctly. After the threshold is raised it answers only 5 of them and gets 4 right, so accuracy on what was answered goes from 50% to 80%; but coverage falls from 100% to 50%. Reporting only the 80% and letting users think the capability has improved across the board is not allowed. The cost of handing the remaining 5 questions to a person or to a stronger model has to be counted too.

The local test reports overall accuracy 0.5, post-selection accuracy 0.8 and coverage 0.5 together. These are synthetic labels, not a measured score for a commercial LLM.

The threshold should be chosen by risk, not by chasing the highest accuracy

For a high-risk action, lower coverage in exchange for fewer errors is acceptable; for ordinary reading Q&A, coverage and explaining the uncertainty may matter more. Build a risk—coverage curve, and note the cost of an error, the cost of abstaining and the capacity for human takeover. Keep the calibration set separate from the evaluation set, so the threshold is not tuned on the same data whose final result is then reported.

Calibration can stop holding once the distribution changes

When the old tasks were mostly short FAQs and the new ones become long-text reasoning or a new domain, the same confidence score may no longer correspond to the same rate of correctness. Monitor performance broken down by task type, length, source status and version; do not fix one calibration in place forever.

Follow-up: if the softmax maximum is 0.9, can the answer be called 90% correct?

No. It corresponds to the model's distribution over the candidate tokens at one step, and is also affected by temperature and candidate truncation; a complete answer may contain several propositions that can be wrong. Define the scoring and the calibration against the target outcome; a local generation probability must not be passed off as factual reliability.

Follow-up: is a model safer the more it abstains?

Abstaining too much fails the task as well. An evaluation should look at wrongly letting something through and at needless abstention together, and confirm whether a reasonable search was made before abstaining. A reliable system is not one that never speaks, but one that knows where the evidence falls short, which parts it can answer, and how to verify the next step.[EVAL]

Q102｜Why does self-reflection sometimes make things worse with each revision? How is effective feedback designed?

What to know first for this question An image is encoded into a numeric representation; a patch is a slice of the image, and one slice does not necessarily equal one final token; the interface budget depends on the implementation.
Lesson 36 · A row of numbers, a table and a stack of tablesLesson 42 · The arithmetic a model does mostLesson 46 · Attention step by stepLesson 64 · Multimodality and trustworthiness

Core answer

Self-reflection can reorganize the reasoning or find an explicit contradiction, but without new evidence it may also repeat the same blind spot and wrongly overturn a correct answer. Effective feedback should point concretely at a checkable error and at an external result, rather than vaguely asking it to "think it over more carefully".[REACT][EVALS]

Looking at feedback quality through one repair loop

The model wrote a sorting function, and the test shows that elements with equal keys change order. The low-quality feedback is "the code is not good enough, please improve it"; the high-quality feedback is the input, the expectation, the actual output and the stability requirement. The latter narrows the space of candidate errors, so the model can check the comparator and the sorting algorithm instead of rewriting unrelated modules.

Feedback also has to establish that it is itself trustworthy. A test log from an old version cannot prove the new code is wrong; a comment generated by a model may misread the requirement. A feedback record should carry the version, the command that was run and the failure conditions. After a fix, accept only the result of a fresh run, so that a natural-language "it has been changed" is not taken as grounds for closing the defect.

The stopping condition matters just as much: the same failure signature appearing again and again means the current approach is making no new progress, so change the evidence or state plainly that you are blocked. Endless self-review inflates the cost and adds unrelated changes as well. For a small problem, fixing it during review and verifying it is usually more sensible than reopening the whole analysis, but the scope of the change and an independent acceptance still have to be kept.

Effective reflection needs new evidence; repeated self-assessment may only rewrite the error

Conditions for this example: an independent arithmetic task: the first answer claims 2+3=6. If the model's self-check still has no basis for computing, it may repeat 6 in a different tone; an external deterministic computation returns 5.

How to read the changes in the figure

Reflection is not useless; what is needed is knowing on what grounds it changes a judgment. External feedback rules out wrong paths better than a vague demand to "be more careful".

A verifier has a scope too: an arithmetic check does not verify a business premise, and a unit test proves nothing about a requirement it does not cover.

Cap loops that make no progress, keep the methods that already failed along with the evidence, and do not let the same mistake be repackaged as a new attempt in different words.

Do not conflate: a "the test passed" narrative the model invents for itself is not external feedback and cannot count as successful verification.

Follow-up: is an independent reviewer always more reliable than self-assessment?

Not necessarily. If it is given the same wrong summary, the same model and the same missing evidence, its failures will still be correlated. What makes something independent is a different evidence path, an executable check and an explicit standard — not naming another Agent"Reviewer".

Follow-up: when the model points out a potential problem, should defensive code be added at once?

Establish the real triggering conditions, the blast radius and the constraints already in place first. A boundary you can imagine in theory is not a risk the current system has to handle. Piling up defenses with no evidence may change the semantics and add complexity, introducing defects instead.

Follow-up: can the result of a reflection be stored directly as experience?

It should be saved as a candidate carrying its evidence status. An explanation given after a task fails may be no more than rationalization after the event; only once the source, a test or a verifiable result supports it is it fit to be promoted to reusable guidance. The scope it applies to and the counterexamples should be kept with it.

Q103｜How does a multimodal model feed an image into a language model? And what extra links does a GUI agent add?

Core answer

Start with the objects and symbols used here
CLIP and image-text contrastive learning
Contrastive learning uses an objective that makes paired representations easier to match and keeps unpaired ones relatively distinct. CLIP-style training learns the association between image and text; it does not automatically provide full chat generation or reliable desktop operation. One image with its correct caption can serve as a positive pair and the other captions as the contrast; training for relevance does not mean every generated conclusion is factually correct.

A common multimodal architecture uses a vision encoder to turn an image into features, which a projection, cross-attention or some other fusion structure then makes available to the language model; the exact design is not uniform. Outside the model, a GUI agent also needs screenshot capture, a mapping to coordinates or elements, execution of the operation, and observation of the result. An image entering the context does not mean the model holds a live screen or permission to operate it.[CLIP][OSWORLD]

The execution chain from pixel to action

Start with the objects and symbols used here
Patch, image channels and the visual projection
A patch in an image is a small block cut out of it, and RGB is the 3 channels red, green and blue; a patch in software engineering is a code patch — the same word for both. An image block, once flattened, can become a vector through a learnable transformation; it is not a lookup in a text vocabulary. Independent image example: one 16×16×3 block holds 768 numbers, and after projection it can become d features. d is fixed by the model design.

One teaching architecture cuts the image up, one patch at a time, converting each patch into a vector, having a vision Transformer produce the features, and then projecting them to a dimension the language side can use. The resolution, the cropping and the patch layout affect how much detail can be kept; if the text is too small or a key control falls outside the crop, no amount of later reasoning can use information that is not there.

At the core of CLIP is contrastive-learning alignment between image and text representations; by itself it is not a complete chat model that can answer about an image with text of any length. Feeding a visual representation into a generative language model takes the matching connection and training as well. How different products bill image tokens, encode internally and handle high resolution cannot be inferred from the interface alone.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
IDE, CLI, UI and DOM
IDE is the integrated development environment, CLI the command-line interface, UI the user interface, and DOM the tree of structural objects of a web document. The code, selection and page information that each entry point attaches automatically can differ. The model seeing the selected function in the IDE does not mean the same task in the CLI has received that function; check this turn's input objects first.

A GUI system first captures a screenshot at time t, the model picks an element or a coordinate, and the executor operates at t+Δ. If the page reflows during Δ, a correct recognition can still click the wrong thing. The executor can therefore use the DOM or the accessibility tree as well, to locate a stable object, and observe again after a critical action. The screenshot, the element ID, the zoom and the scroll offset should all belong to the same version of the observation.

Image-block vectors and text embeddings come from different inputs, yet can feed into the same computation chain

Follow-up: with the DOM available, is a screenshot still needed?

The DOM gives structure and text, but may not fully express visual occlusion, canvas content and what is actually visible; a screenshot gives the visual, but locating a semantic object in it may be imprecise. The two are complementary, and the choice depends on the environment. The DOM being there is no reason to overlook a covered button, and pixels alone are no basis for guessing that a stable element identifier already exists.

Follow-up: do multimodal inputs have prompt injection too?

Yes. Untrusted text can appear in a screenshot, an image or an interface. Once the text has been recognized, its source level still has to be kept; a command inside an image must not be taken as authorization from the user. The trust boundary of a visual input is the same as that of a text tool result.

Follow-up: why can a vision model's confidence not replace confirming the action?

Visual confidence is about "what was seen"; it does not prove "what the business state is after the operation". Whether a submission, a deletion or a payment took effect has to be observed in the result or queried from the system. That the object confirmed and the object acted on are different things is the core of GUI reliability.

Engineering mechanisms and failure boundaries

The interface was read correctly — so why can the action still click the wrong thing?

A screenshot is also evidence with a version

In teaching interface r7 the "Save" button is at coordinates (700,400). After the model observes it, a dialog appears and turns the layout into r8. Clicking on at the old coordinates may trigger a different action. A screen coordinate is not a permanent object identity, and a page's DOM selector can also stop working after a re-render.

The smallest observe—act—verify loop

First record the current interface, window, zoom and scroll; confirm the target of the operation and the permissions; perform one minimal action; observe the actual change again. A successful click proves only that the event was sent, not that data was saved or submitted at the back end. Verify the status text, the request result or a downstream record — but an optimistic UI message must not be taken for an irreversible business success.

How to choose between an API and a GUI

Where a reliable, authorized, clearly structured API exists, the arguments and the result are usually easier to control; operating the interface suits a process that has to be completed through the human interface. It is neither "a browser is more like a person, so it is smarter" nor "an API is certainly safe". Both routes need a trust boundary, management of side effects, and evidence.

A model's confidence differs from the rate of factual correctness

A token probability over the vocabulary is the model's distribution, not a calibrated true probability that the whole answer is right. An attention heat map, too, is part of the internal computation rather than a complete causal explanation. Citing evidence, stating the uncertainty and running a checkable verification are more reliable than having the model repeat "I am quite certain".

Q104｜Can research on model interpretability really tell us "why it answered that way"?

What to know first for this question Optimizing against feedback may teach the model to please the grader instead of really improving the task, so the explainable process, the real result and the reward metric have to be kept apart.
Lesson 54 · The mathematics needed before post-trainingLesson 05 · The denominator of an evaluation metricLesson 31 · Do not be fooled by a few percentLesson 64 · Multimodality and trustworthiness

Core answer

Start with the objects and symbols used here
SAE, activations and interpretable features
SAE stands for sparse autoencoder: it reconstructs a model's activations from a combination of relatively few active features. An activation is an intermediate number from this computation, not a complete record of natural-language reasoning. A successful reconstruction does not by itself prove that a feature has a unique causal meaning. Seeing a feature change together with the output is a clue; judging causation also takes control of other conditions, intervention and a comparison.

Interpretability provides methods for studying internal representations and causal mechanisms, but one activation correlation, one attention weight or one readable reasoning text cannot fully explain the model's decision. Observational, interventional and generalization evidence have to be kept apart, so that a local phenomenon is not expanded into a complete account of the whole system.[SAE][TR]

From activation to feature, and on to a causal test

A language model's hidden vector may represent several concepts superposed. A sparse autoencoder tries to reconstruct the activation from a larger number of sparse features: the encoder produces sparse coefficients and the decoder's linear combination approximates the original vector, with the sparsity constrained at the same time. You can check which texts a given feature activates on, but calling it the "programming error feature" is only a summary of the observation, not a label with inherent meaning.

A feature can then be suppressed, amplified or replaced, and the change in the output observed. That comes closer to causal evidence than correlation alone, but an intervention may depart from the natural activation distribution and may affect several behaviors at once. Reconstruction error, feature splitting, uncaptured directions and the choice of data all limit the conclusion.

For engineering diagnosis, checking the observable input, the tool results, the version and the tests first is usually more practical than tracing neurons directly. Research into internal explanation cannot replace business acceptance; equally, a model-generated "I did this because …" is another piece of model output, with no guarantee that it faithfully records the internal computation.

An attention heat map is an observation; only causal intervention begins to test the explanation

Conditions for this example: an independent research proposal: when a certain answer appears, the model's attention to position j is very high. The other conditions are then kept as similar as possible while the input is replaced or the internal activation is intervened on, and the change in output is observed. No experimental results are fabricated here.

Follow-up: is the token with the highest attention weight the reason for the answer?

That cannot be inferred directly. The output also passes through the Value projection, several heads, residuals, the MLP and later layers; a high weight is not the only contribution, and low-weight information may also affect the output by another path. A more systematic attribution or intervention experiment is needed.

Follow-up: once a feature for dangerous behavior is found, can the risk be removed once and for all?

There is no guarantee. The behavior may be spread across several features and paths, and an intervention may also damage normal capability. Evaluation across several situations and a defensive layer are needed; one internal feature must not be treated as a complete safety switch.

Follow-up: why is interpretability still worth learning?

It helps tell "a behavior was observed" apart from "the mechanism that produced it is understood", and it provides methods for studying a model's representations and causal effects. For a user, the most important gain is recognizing how strong or weak a piece of explanatory evidence is, rather than memorizing a batch of feature names.

Engineering deep dive · E54 · continues Q108

Why is the brightest position in an attention heat map not necessarily the main reason for the answer?

Prerequisite: Lesson 46 · Lesson 49 · Lesson 64. Reading order: confirm the run contract first, then look at the counterexamples and the fix, and finally test the limits.

The share read and the final contribution are different objects

In a one-dimensional teaching aggregation with weights [0.9,0.1] and Value [0,100], the contributions are [0,10]. The first position, which has the largest attention, contributes 0, while the second supplies the entire aggregated value. A more complex network also has multi-head projections, residuals, nonlinearities and later layers. Looking at the size of the weights alone therefore cannot explain the final output directly.[SDPA]

This small counterexample in the book proves only that "the largest weight does not entail the largest numeric contribution"; it does not claim to have carried out causal attribution on a real model. Real interpretability research has to trace internal paths, construct interventions and acknowledge the scope within which its results apply.[CIRCUIT]

Where a correlational visualization and a causal experiment differ

A visualization shows a correlated quantity from the run at hand; a causal question asks "what happens if this is changed while the other conditions are held constant". Masking a token, replacing an activation or switching off a head can each affect several paths, and can even push the model outside its training distribution. Observing a change does not by itself prove that this is the only cause during a natural run.

A more reliable experiment uses controlled inputs, an explicit object of intervention and comparable output metrics, and checks for alternative paths and side effects. Ablating an attention head and changing the input text are not equivalent interventions, and not every change can be attributed to one highlighted block.

Self-reported reasoning cannot replace internal evidence either

A model can give a coherent explanation, but whether that text is faithful to the real computation needs separate verification. Finding some of the paths in research is not the same as being able to read the model's thought in full. Do not treat the "thinking process" a user sees as an engineering log or as proof of execution.

Follow-up: so is a heat map of no use at all?

It is. It can help find a mask bug, an abnormal concentration or a position offset, and can guide a research hypothesis. But its use should be written up as "diagnosing an intermediate computation or generating a hypothesis", not as "proving why the model answered this way". The more intuitive a figure, the more its subject needs stating.

Follow-up: how is a teaching animation kept from misleading?

In the animation, label the input, the weights, the Value, the output and the simplifying conditions in step; recompute with pinned parameters; say which layers were left out. Keep real model data separate from a hand-made sketch. A handsome figure should not draw a mechanism that is not yet known as a definite causal chain.
