Q109｜What do Scaling Laws and Chinchilla actually show? Why can one token/parameter ratio not simply be memorized?

What to know first for this question A scaling law is an empirical relationship under a particular setting, not a law of unlimited extrapolation; parameters, data, compute and quality together decide the result.
Lesson 53 · One training run end to endLesson 61 · Low rank, quantization, expertsLesson 62 · Computing fast and fitting in memoryLesson 31 · Do not be fooled by a few percent

Core answer

Scaling laws study how model size, data and compute budget affect the loss; compute-optimal allocation asks how, under a given budget and set of assumptions, to divide that budget between parameters and training Token. It is an empirical fit and an optimization problem, not a proportionality law that holds constant for every architecture, every data quality and every deployment goal.[SCALING][CHINCHILLA]

Deriving the budget allocation from a simplified objective

Suppose the loss can be approximated as L=E+A/N^α+B/D^β, where N is the parameter count and D the number of training tokens. Suppose further that the training compute is C=kND, so D=C/(kN), and substituting gives:

L(N)=E + A·N^(−α) + B·(kN/C)^β

Differentiating with respect to N and setting the result to zero gives:

αA·N^(−α) = βB·(k/C)^β·N^β
N* = [αA/(βB)]^(1/(α+β)) · (C/k)^(β/(α+β))
D* = C/(kN*)

This shows that the optimal allocation depends on the fitted coefficients, the exponents and the budget model. If the data repeats, the quality changes, or the training recipe or the architecture changes, the original fit need not continue to apply. If the real objective includes a large amount of later inference cost, training a smaller model for longer may suit the total life-cycle cost better than merely minimizing the loss of one training run.

Do not take an improvement in the pre-training loss as directly equivalent to a rise in some Agent task's success rate. In between come post-training, tool adaptation, retrieval, execution and verification. A model with lower perplexity may still be poor at a particular protocol or at high-risk operations.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
FLOPs and FLOP/s: a count and a rate
FLOPs usually means a number of floating-point operations; FLOP/s means a rate of operations per second. Papers and vendors may count a multiply-add as one operation or two, so the basis has to be fixed. Dividing total operations by the theoretical peak is no promise of real elapsed time: movement, queueing, communication and actual utilization cost something too.

A scaling law is a fit under given conditions, not a recipe that always applies

How to read the changes in the figure

The formula approximates cost; it is not a formula for quality. The same 6ND can still reach different losses with different data, optimization and architecture.

Optimal training compute and optimal total deployment cost are not the same objective: when the number of calls in production is large, the inference bill affects the choice of model size.

When reading a report, look at the scope of the study and the measured points first and at the extrapolated curve afterwards; the ratio of one period cannot be taken as a universal constant.

Do not conflate: treating an empirical regularity as an exact law of physics produces the wrong budget under a new architecture, repeated data or a different training length.

Worked example · Q109

The training budget is fixed and the parameters are doubled, so why may the data halve

Limits and counterexamples. C≈6ND is a rough estimate under particular assumptions; it ignores part of the attention, embedding and similar overheads, and makes no claim to reproduce the exact optimal ratio of any paper.

Follow-up: is the formula C≈6ND exact?

Start with the objects and symbols used here
MoE, experts and the router
MoE is a model structure that contains several sub-networks; the router selects or weights a few experts for the current token's representation. An expert is usually a numeric sub-network, not a chat agent given a prompt such as "math teacher". Independent example: with 4 experts and 2 chosen per token, one pass activates only 2 of the expert paths; the parameters and routing state that the model design requires still have to be stored.

It is an order-of-magnitude approximation commonly used under particular dense-Transformer training conditions, and it ignores or simplifies part of the attention, embedding and communication costs. Long contexts, MoE and different implementations change the real cost. Capacity planning has to go back to the specific operators and to hardware measurement.

Follow-up: is data of the same token count worth the same?

No. Repetition, noise, domain coverage, quality and mixing proportions all affect how much is effectively learned. Scaling up poor data is no guarantee of being equivalent to adding independent high-quality information. Test contamination also has to be kept from making the capability look stronger than it is.

Follow-up: a company has only a little private data — should it train a foundation model?

Usually compare first whether retrieval, prompting, tools, skills or fine-tuning are enough. The data, compute and verification that foundation pre-training requires go far beyond writing one domain prompt. Whether the real need is up-to-date knowledge or behavior adaptation decides which layer to change; the word "private" does not on its own imply training from scratch.

Engineering deep dive · E55 · continues Q109

Why compute-optimal training is not necessarily cheapest over a product's whole life cycle

Prerequisite: Lesson 61 · Lesson 62 · Lesson 33. Reading order: confirm the run contract first, then the counterexamples and the fix, and test the limits last.

First be clear about which budget "optimal" holds fixed

The Chinchilla study compares how model size and data volume are allocated under a given training compute.[SCALE] It is not simultaneously optimizing, for every product, the latency, VRAM and serving cost of a hundred million requests after launch. Memorizing some training-data/parameter ratio as an eternal law loses the conditions of the study.

A product's total cost includes at least training, data, deployment, inference, maintenance and rework after failures. A model that is more expensive to train but cheaper to run can end up with a lower total cost once the request volume is large enough; conversely, a low-traffic experiment is not necessarily worth a large training outlay.

An explicit break-even calculation

Suppose approach B spends 1000 units more on training than A and saves 0.01 per request, with everything else the same for now. It takes 100000 requests to recover the difference. Below that number it is more expensive; only above it can it be cheaper. The figures are teaching algebra only, not current API prices or a result from a paper; where the quality is not equal, the cost of failures and of human work has to be added as well.

Why fewer parameters still does not guarantee faster inference

Real latency depends on sequence length, batch, memory bandwidth, communication, kernel support and scheduling. The KV preemption and prefill policies in the vLLM tuning documentation are examples of influence at the system layer.[SCHED] Taking the model's parameter count on its own as a prediction of latency misses the serving bottleneck.

Data volume is likewise not the only variable: repeated text, diversity, quality and the match to the target domain all change how well learning works. "Doubling the training tokens" is not doubling the independent information, and an empirical curve cannot be applied without looking at what the data is made of.

Follow-up: once a study's conclusion is out of date, can it no longer be used?

An older study can still supply method: control the variables, compare budget allocations, fit and then verify. But when the architecture, the data and the hardware change, the empirical constants and the optimum have to be re-estimated. Cite the study's conditions when using it, and do not write the settings of that year into a product's defaults.

Follow-up: how do you run an engineering decision experiment that is small enough?

Fix the task set and the quality floor, then compare a few candidates on cost per success, p95 and resources under the real input/output length distribution; after that, use the projected traffic to compute the life-cycle range. Rule out the approaches that clearly fail the quality or memory constraints first; there is no need to build a large model-routing platform at the outset.

Q110｜What is the essential difference between MLA and GQA? Why can a low-rank KV reduce the cache?

What to know first for this question MLA compresses a cacheable latent representation and GQA shares K/V heads; the difference lies in the transformation and in the cache structure. Do not treat them as the same method just because VRAM goes down.
Lesson 42 · The arithmetic a model does mostLesson 46 · Attention step by stepLesson 58 · KV cache explained from scratchLesson 61 · Low rank, quantization, experts

Core answer

Start with the objects and symbols used here
Query, Key, Value: three intermediate numeric representations
q is one row of query representation for the position receiving information; k is one row of matching representation offered by a readable position; v is one row of content representation that position contributes to the mix. Capital Q/K/V collect several rows into tables. They are computed from the input and the parameters, and they carry the separate jobs of scoring and of supplying content. One q dotted with two k gives two scores; softmax turns them into two shares; those shares multiply the two rows of v, and the results are added. Nothing here generates a search term, and nothing here picks an answer directly.
MHA, MQA, GQA: how heads are shared
An attention head is one path that matches and then aggregates content. MHA usually gives every query head its own K/V head; MQA has several query heads share one group of K/V; GQA sits between them, sharing by group. These are structural choices, not a switch to be flipped at run time. Independent shape example: 8 query heads and 2 K/V heads, grouped evenly, means every 4 query heads share 1 group of K/V; the cache is accounted for by the number of K/V heads.
Start with the objects and symbols used here
MLA: a learned low-dimensional K/V representation
MLA refers to a family of multi-head latent attention architectures. It organizes the content behind K/V jointly in a lower-dimensional latent representation and handles the positional part by design. It changes the model's representation and parameter structure; it is not just ZIPping an ordinary KV file. In c=xW_down, c is the latent vector for this computation and W_down the learned table of dimension-reducing coefficients; how it is later reconstructed or absorbed into a projection has to be explained for the specific architecture.

GQA reduces the cache by having several Query heads share fewer K/V heads; MLA expresses K/V jointly through a learned low-dimensional latent representation and gives the positional part a dedicated design. MLA is part of the model architecture and of its training; compressing the KV of an arbitrary existing model after the fact is no guarantee of equivalence.[GQA][V3]

Understanding a low-rank representation through matrix multiplication

Start with the objects and symbols used here
SwiGLU, Wg/Wu/Wd: two transformation paths, then combined
This is a gated feed-forward structure. Wg produces the path that goes through SiLU, Wu produces the other content path, the two are multiplied element by element, and Wd maps the result back to the original width. These Ws are three sets of parameters, not three agents. To read (SiLU(XWg)⊙XWu)Wd, compute XWg and XWu first, then SiLU, then ⊙, and finally multiply by Wd. Every position uses the same set of parameters independently.

Using the column-vector convention, let the hidden vector at some position be h, and first compute c=W_down h, where the dimension d_c of c is smaller than the dimension of all the K/V heads expanded out. The content Key and Value can be expressed as k_i=W_upK,i c, v_i=W_upV,i c. Considering only the content score:

q_iᵀ k_i = q_iᵀ W_upK,i c = (W_upK,iᵀ q_i)ᵀ c

So the Query can be transformed into the latent space and then scored against the cached c, which avoids always keeping the full K explicitly. For the Value weighted sum there is likewise:

Σ: sum term by term over the subscript
Σ is shorthand for addition. The subscript says which term varies; a subscript that is not summed over stays fixed. It is not an extra parameter, and it does not mean concatenating arrays. Independent micro-example: Σᵢ xᵢyᵢ with x=[1,2] and y=[3,4] is just 1×3+2×4=11.
Σ_j a_ij v_j,i = W_upV,i · (Σ_j a_ij c_j)

Linear transformations can be combined where it suits, but softmax cannot be moved around or linearized at will. The MLA of DeepSeek-V3 also separates the position-dependent Key from the content part, caching the compressed latent vector together with the corresponding positional Key; the Query too has a content part and a positional part. The actual sizes and the order of the operators should follow the architecture's implementation, and the ordinary MHA cache formula should not be carried over to it.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
LoRA, low rank and r
LoRA represents part of a weight increment as the product of two smaller matrices instead of retraining the whole large matrix. r is the inner dimension, and it bounds the maximum rank of that product. Where sources use a row-vector or a column-vector convention, the left-right order of A and B can differ. Micro-example in the right-multiplication convention: with W[4,6], the increment A[4,2]B[2,6] is still [4,6]; r=2. A here is not the table of attention shares.

The equations above explain why there is room to rearrange the linear part; they do not prove that a low-rank architecture produces exactly the same output as any full MHA model. A low-rank restriction needs training to adapt to it; operator fusion and cache layout also affect the real gain. A smaller cache does not automatically mean lower latency at every batch and context length.

Follow-up: why can RoPE not simply be absorbed into one fixed projection?

RoPE depends on position. Mixing the positional rotation together with the content up-projection usually makes the transformation vary with position, so one fixed matrix cannot simply be reused at every past position. Decoupling the positional branch keeps position modeling while retaining the convenience of a low-rank cache.

Follow-up: is MLA just KV quantization?

No. Quantization reduces the number of bits used to represent each number; MLA changes the dimension and the structure of the representation that has to be cached. The two can be combined in principle, but the error, the operator support and the effect have to be verified separately: the gain from one technique cannot simply be multiplied by the advertised factor of another.

Follow-up: which layer does each of MoE, MLA and FlashAttention change?

Start with the objects and symbols used here
FlashAttention: changing how intermediate data is stored and read
By means such as tiling and online normalization, it avoids keeping the full attention score/weight matrix in slower VRAM for long; the target is still the same attention computation, and floating-point rounding can vary with the order of operations. It does not compress tokens permanently, and it does not skip any Key. Keep "not writing the whole table to VRAM" apart from "not doing this arithmetic at all".

MoE changes the sparse selection in the feed-forward computation; MLA changes the attention representation; FlashAttention optimizes the IO and the intermediate storage of the attention computation. They address different bottlenecks, can appear in the same system, and are not equivalent to one another.

Q111｜Is In-Context Learning really "learning"? Why does changing a few examples change the behavior?

What to know first for this question In-context learning is a pattern shown under the current input conditions; it does not mean the parameters are updated each time. Complex capability comes from training and inference acting together.
Lesson 37 · Why text has to become token IDs firstLesson 45 · The smallest neural network modelLesson 53 · One training run end to endLesson 11 · Assembling context

Core answer

In ordinary inference the model parameters are not updated, but the examples in the input change the hidden state and the conditional distribution, so task adaptation shows up. This is called in-context learning; it differs from training, which updates parameters by gradients, and from writing experience into an external long-term memory.[GPT3][TR]

Tracking the change through a label-mapping example

Suppose the input gives the examples "positive review → label A, negative review → label B". Once the model has taken these examples in, the later positions use the correspondence in them through attention; the logits of the final classification token are produced from a hidden state that contains this information. Once the examples are deleted or the session is reset, this local mapping may no longer be supplied, because the weights were not permanently changed by that request.

The choice of examples affects the task the model infers: whether the labels are balanced, whether the format is consistent, whether the examples cover the boundaries, and whether their order creates a bias. If every example uses the same label, the model may learn the output format as one fixed answer. Wrong examples can contaminate the current behavior as well. Good few-shot work is not piling examples up; it is supplying information that conveys the task rules, the boundaries and the output contract.

"The model remembers my project" calls for a further question: is the current context still carrying the information, has the client reloaded the files, has the retrieval system fetched the memory back, or has the model really been fine-tuned? Only by telling these mechanisms apart can recovery, deletion and privacy controls be designed correctly.

In-context learning changes the conditions of this run; it does not change the weight file

Conditions for this example: an independent classification prompt: two examples are given, "good → positive" and "bad → negative", and then "excellent" is asked about. The model's parameters are unchanged, but a task rule has been added to the input.

Putting the same function under different input conditions changes the output, and that does not require the function itself to have changed. This explains "it used the format as soon as I taught it".

This does not deny that the model performs complex adaptation internally; it states the observable mechanism: ordinary inference does not thereby run weight optimization.

The order of the examples, the quality of the labels and the task distribution can all affect the result; a few examples are no guarantee of having learned something permanently, across tasks.

Do not conflate: calling a correction made in chat permanent training easily leads users to believe that a new session keeps every rule automatically.

Follow-up: does every conversation update the vendor's model parameters?

That cannot be inferred. A single inference, a product's memory and a vendor's offline training are different processes; the data-use policy depends on the particular product and configuration, and a model "remembering a sentence" is no basis for inferring that it has updated weights online. This book does not speculate about undisclosed data-handling implementations.

Follow-up: the longer the context and the more few-shot examples, the better?

More examples can help with coverage, and can also bring in redundancy, conflict and position effects. The number of examples, the selection strategy and the cost have to be compared on a fixed task set. If the error comes from a missing fact about the code, adding format examples is not necessarily useful; what needs filling in is the evidence.

Follow-up: how are a skill and few-shot related?

Skill can contain instructions, examples, resources and scripts; once loaded, these become part of the context or of the tool support. It does not change the parameters by itself, and it does not guarantee that the model follows it strictly. The value of a skill has to be verified through task behavior, not confirmed by the file existing.

Q112｜Faced with a new framework, a new paper or an agent failure, how do you judge which layer to change?

What to know first for this question Diagnose layer by layer through input, model, tools, state, evidence and verification, and choose the smallest effective fix; do not add retrieval and review without limit just because you are uncertain.
Lesson 02 · State, events and checkpointsLesson 17 · From search results to a trustworthy answerLesson 26 · Retries, idempotence and transactionsLesson 04 · Foundations of engineering qualityLesson 05 · The denominator of an evaluation metricLesson 33 · Reading a production runLesson 66 · From core concepts to system analysis

Core answer

First locate whether the failure happens in the information, the model, the protocol, the execution, the state or the acceptance, and only then choose the smallest measure that can change the cause. Papers and frameworks offer candidate mechanisms; they do not replace causal diagnosis. An engineering change has to keep a baseline and a counterexample, to prove that complexity has not been used to cover the problem up.[EVALS][HARNESS]

Working backwards from one wrong answer

Step one: confirm the task definition — what exactly did the user ask for, and how is success judged? Step two: see whether the evidence exists, whether there is permission for it, and whether the version is right. Step three: see whether the evidence was retrieved and reached the final model request; "a tool read it" is not "the current model still sees it". Step four: check whether the model output expresses the action correctly, and whether the protocol and the arguments are complete.

Step five: verify that the external execution and the persistent state agree, especially around timeouts and interruptions. Step six: check that the verification targets the final version and that it really ran. Only when these conditions are broadly right and the failure still occurs reliably is there better reason to attribute the bottleneck to model capability and to try a stronger model, a task split or post-training.

When reading a new paper, join up "the problem the authors solve — the key assumptions — the object changed — the experimental conditions — the limitations". If a study is about question answering over static documents yet is used to show that dynamic code modification is reliable, the scope has been stretched much too far. If a new framework only wraps existing mechanisms in layers of classes without producing a gain in task quality or efficiency, it is not necessarily worth moving to.

Find the earliest error boundary first, then decide whether to change the model or change the system

Conditions for this example: an independent agent failure: the final answer is "production timeout=10", while the real rule is 30. Possible causes are a retrieval that missed a condition, compaction that dropped "test", a misuse in generation, or stale tool state.

Follow-up: how do you avoid making a simple task complicated?

For every retriever, agent, review or memory layer added, first state the specific failure that happens without it and the improvement that can be observed. If neither can be stated, keep the simple implementation for now. The principle is that complexity is driven by real evidence and risk, not by the completeness of a catalog.

Follow-up: how do you avoid over-simplifying a complex task?

Check the cross-module invariants, the unknown side effects, the permission boundaries and the irreversible operations. While these are still unclear, changing one line directly may only hide the risk. Simple does not mean fewer steps; it means no useless steps. The analysis and verification that are needed should be kept.

Follow-up: how do you tell whether you have really learned it?

Given a task you have not seen before, you can explain step by step the objects, the data flow, the key state, the failure window and the way it is verified, and you can point out which conclusions have no evidence yet. Being able to describe the run clearly, locate an error to a layer and verify the gain from a fix comes far closer to real ability than reciting terms.

Engineering deep dive · E56 · continues Q112

Deep troubleshooting does not end at "switch to a stronger model" but at finding the invariant that failed

Prerequisite: Lesson 04 · Lesson 05 · Lesson 33 · Lesson 66. Reading order: confirm the run contract first, then the counterexamples and the fix, and test the limits last.

Breaking the system into a causal chain you can intervene in

For one failure, write down the user's acceptance result first, then locate it along the chain of input evidence, retrieval, context assembly, model output, tool execution, state write-back and verification. At every layer ask: which invariant should it preserve, and where is that actually broken? "The model is not clever enough" is too broad to guide a smallest fix.

A controlled oracle substitution can be used to diagnose: give the model the genuinely correct evidence directly, to test whether retrieval is the bottleneck; hand the executor tool arguments that are known to be correct, to check the execution boundary; hand the verifier the final artifact directly, to check whether the grader misjudges it. These substitutions are for locating a problem; a success rate under oracle conditions must not be taken as a deployment score. The principles of real outcomes and of infrastructure control in agent evaluation support this layered comparison.[EVAL] [NOISE]

No gain from a single factor may still hide an interaction

This book's teaching table: old retrieval + old prompt gives a success rate of 0.6, new retrieval + old prompt 0.6, old retrieval + new prompt 0.6, and only both new together gives 0.9. The interaction term is 0.9−0.6−0.6+0.6=0.3. This is not measured product data; it illustrates that new evidence only works once the new assembly logic uses it correctly, and that looking at an independent A/B of one module alone can miss a combined dependency.

Conversely, an improvement after changing five things at once tells you nothing about which one helped. Run a limited set of combinations based on the dependency you suspect, and avoid the pointless expense of a full permutation. Start from real failure samples and the smallest falsifiable hypothesis.

How much is changed, and the risk, are part of the conclusion too

If inverting one mask fixes every related counterexample, there is no need to rewrite the whole agent; if the underlying permission model is wrong, one more sentence of prompt is not enough. Judging which layer to fix has to follow the evidence, not a preference for a more complex architecture. Acceptance covers the intended fix, neighboring regressions, the cost and any new failure modes.

Follow-up: how do you write troubleshooting experience into memory without overfitting?

Record the trigger condition, the failure mechanism, the original evidence, the smallest fix, the versions it holds for, the counterexample and the conditions under which it stops working. Do not write only broad instructions such as "from now on, add more context whenever there is a problem". When the environment differs next time, check the premises first, and do not promote one success into an eternal rule.

Follow-up: what is the minimum a "senior-level canned answer" should contain?

First define the objects and the boundaries accurately; then walk through the key state or computation; give a counterexample that separates a correct implementation from a wrong one; explain why the fix works, what it costs and under which conditions it stops working; and finally be able to point to the basis and the verification path. Reciting terms, flow charts or a list of "best practices" is not enough to answer a series of follow-ups.

The final acceptance question

Given a failure trace, can you name the cheapest and most discriminating check to do next? If the answer is always "rerun it, change the model, add more material, add one more review agent", then the mechanisms learned have not yet been turned into engineering judgment.

Engineering mechanisms and failure boundaries

How is a knowledge graph turned into engineering judgment rather than a compendium of concepts?

The smallest usable unit of the graph is a relationship you can check

Stringing the three words "agent—tool—memory" together carries almost no information. More useful edges are:模型提议 --必须经校验--> 执行器, 后置钩子 --不回滚--> 已完成副作用, 压缩候选 --依赖版本--> 历史快照. Each edge notes its source, the versions it applies to, and a counterexample.

Putting the problem back into the graph to locate it

An agent that keeps modifying the same file may be a result that was not filled back in, a task state that was not persisted, a summary that turned "done" into "not done", an unstable retry key, or a subtask dispatched twice. Start from the visible symptom and follow the dependency edges to find the break. Simply adding a prompt that says "do not repeat yourself" only hides the mechanism problem.

Three different kinds of conclusion

Fact: in the source, a certain function handles the post-execution hook after the handler returns. Inference: a refusal at that point cannot stop an external action that has already happened. Recommendation: a sensitive operation should be checked before execution, and an unknown result should be given a verification or idempotent path. The three have to be kept apart; a recommendation must not be written up as a guarantee the product already provides.

How to use the graph when making a change

Change a tool schema, and argument assembly, hook rewriting, authorization binding, execution and filling the result back in all need regression. Change compaction, and the key constraints, the historical versions, the calls in flight and recovery need regression. Change memory, and extraction, leases, artifact verification, source withdrawal and expiry at the point of use need regression. The graph helps to settle the scope of the impact; it does not require every small change to rerun every test in the system.

A design that is not worth doing has to be removable too

A mechanism with no matching failure mode and no gain on the evaluation, yet with added calls, state and maintenance cost, should not be kept because it looks complete. Advanced engineering judgment is knowing when a complex mechanism is needed, and equally when simple direct execution is better.[A01][A07]

Every edge of a knowledge graph has a level of evidence too

"ToolRouter contains the visible specification" is a code relationship; "a post-execution hook cannot roll back a side effect that has already happened" is a conclusion about execution order; "add one more check for high-risk operations" is an engineering recommendation. The three kinds of relationship need different verification methods, and they must not all be drawn as the same "official requirement".

This round's graph attaches to each edge a pinned source range, an official documentation section or an original-derivation label, and leaves older records whose original text was not obtained as history rather than continuing to cite them. When reading the graph, look at an edge's predicate and conditions first and at its source afterwards. A passing test supports only the teaching cases that were run; it does not replace reading the source, a Rust integration test, or verification against a commercial service.
