Prerequisites for this chapter

Start with the objects and symbols used here
LoRA, low rank and r
LoRA represents part of a weight increment as the product of two smaller matrices instead of retraining the whole large matrix. r is the inner dimension, and it bounds the maximum rank of that product. Where sources use a row-vector or a column-vector convention, the left-right order of A and B can differ. Micro-example in the right-multiplication convention: with W[4,6], the increment A[4,2]B[2,6] is still [4,6]; r=2. A here is not the table of attention shares.

SFT supplies the target answer, preference training compares answers, and reinforcement learning improves the policy with a reward. Low rank, quantization and expert structures are about how parameters are organized and computed, not about adding a knowledge base to the context.

Q085｜How do SFT, RLHF and DPO each change the model? What does the DPO formula compare?

What to know first for this question SFT imitates the target output, RLHF optimizes the policy with feedback, and DPO compares relative preference probabilities directly; all of them involve training, and none is a prompt added at run time.
Lesson 53 · One training run end to endLesson 54 · The mathematics needed before post-training

Core answer

Start with the objects and symbols used here
RLHF, the reward model and the policy
RLHF refers broadly to processes that train behavior using human feedback. A reward model scores behavior such as an answer; the policy is the probability model that actually produces actions. Their jobs differ, and a high reward is not automatically factual correctness. Independent example: if the reward model prefers long answers, the policy may learn to write at length without being more accurate; the reward criterion therefore needs to be validated.

SFT does supervised learning with target answers; RLHF usually learns or uses a reward signal and then optimizes the policy; DPO builds a training objective directly from pairs of preferred answers to the same question, without running a full online reinforcement-learning loop at every training step. They optimize the distribution of behavior; they are no guarantee of factual truth.[RLHF][DPO]

Starting from two answers to the same question

Given a question x, a preferred answer y⁺ and a dispreferred answer y⁻. SFT takes only one ideal output and raises its probability one Token at a time. It does not tell the model directly "why the other answer is worse"; that distinction is conveyed indirectly by the distribution of the examples.

A reward model can be trained on answer pairs so that r(x,y⁺)>r(x,y⁻). During policy optimization the model generates an answer and is then updated according to the reward, usually together with a penalty on the divergence from the reference policy to avoid violent drift. A reward model only approximates human preference or a task standard, and it may reward surface features.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
sigmoid: maps a single number into the range between 0 and 1
The function rule is sigmoid(x)=1/(1+exp(-x)). It computes number by number and does not require one row of results to sum to 1, so it cannot stand in directly for a softmax over several candidates. Independent micro-example: sigmoid(0)=0.5; computing two zeros separately gives [0.5,0.5], which is only a coincidence — for the input [1,1] the two terms already sum to more than 1.
π, π_ref and β: the symbols of a preference objective
In policy and preference optimization, π denotes a conditional-probability policy and π_ref a fixed reference policy; β is a coefficient that sets the scale of the objective or the strength of the constraint. The probability of a complete answer is built from the conditional probability of each token.π here is not the circle constant, and β is not the LayerNorm offset parameter. The w/l in the DPO expression mark the preferred and the non-preferred answer, not a weight w and a layer count L.
Δ = [log πθ(y⁺|x) − log πref(y⁺|x)]
  − [log πθ(y⁻|x) − log πref(y⁻|x)]
L = −log sigmoid(β Δ)

The probability of an answer is the product of the conditional probabilities of its answer tokens, so in practice the sum of the log probabilities is used. The objective pushes the advantage of the preferred answer over the reference model to be greater than the advantage of the dispreferred answer; it is not a matter of simply setting y⁺ to 1 and y⁻ to 0. The reference model supplies the anchor, and β controls the scale in the objective and is related to the regularization strength in the corresponding derivation.

Suppose the current model and the reference model both give a relative log gain of 0 for the two answers; then Δ=0 and the loss is about 0.693. If the preferred answer has the larger relative gain, Δ grows and the loss falls. This objective still depends on data quality: if the annotators prefer long-winded flattery, training will take that preference as the signal.

What DPO compares is the "preference gain relative to the reference model"

Conditions for this example: an independent pair of answers: the good answer y+ and the poor answer y− are for the same question. The reference model gives the two sequences probabilities 0.2 and 0.1, the current model 0.3 and 0.1; β=1. These probabilities are a teaching setting, chosen to make the formula clear.

Sequence probability
The probability of the whole target under the same condition; in practice it is usually accumulated in logs.
Reference model
Provides the baseline for the relative change.
Preference margin
The relative gain on the good answer minus the relative gain on the bad answer.

How to read the changes in the figure

It is not enough to compare the current model's 0.3 with its 0.1. The formula also asks how much each has changed relative to the reference model.

If both the better and the worse answer are multiplied by the same factor relative to the reference model, the two log gains are equal, the margin between them is 0, and a shared increase brings no extra preference advantage.

A preference label expresses which choice is favoured; it does not automatically prove that the answer is factually correct. Data collection and evaluation still need a factual basis.

Do not conflate: the teaching sequence probabilities are there to show a ratio; real training has to deal with length, valid tokens, numerical stability and the reference distribution, and cannot just multiply many small probabilities together.

Mechanism basis:[DPO] [RLHF]. The numbers and harness setting in this example are an explicitly given teaching construction, not commercial model output or a production performance measurement.

Follow-up: does DPO really need no reward at all?

It does not have to train and call a separate scalar reward model explicitly, but it still needs a preference signal, and an implicit reward relation can be derived from the objective. "No reward model needed" is not "no judgment of quality needed". Likewise, offline preference data cannot cover all the new distributions that appear once the policy has been optimized.

Follow-up: will SFT make the model remember all the new business facts?

It may learn some facts, but it is hard to provide item-by-item updating, deletion and source tracing. Rules or source code that change often are better suited to versioned retrieval; a stable output format, classification boundaries and a style of tool selection can be considered for SFT. A customization decision should first distinguish missing knowledge, unstable behavior, insufficient tools and an unclear task description.

Follow-up: why can preference training make answers more likeable without making them more correct?

Raters easily take fluency, confidence, length or tone as a proxy for quality. Once the model optimizes the proxy, errors that "look professional" can appear. The training data and the evaluation have to cover correctness, citation support, permitted refusal and whether the task was completed, rather than only asking "which passage do you prefer".

Engineering deep dive · E11 · continues Q021

What DPO really compares is not the "probability of the good answer" but how much each has changed relative to the reference policy

Prerequisite: Lesson 53 · Lesson 54. Reading order: confirm the run contract first, then look at the counterexamples and the fix, and finally test the limits.

Identify the four log probabilities first

For the same question x there is a preferred answer y+ and a rejected answer y−; the current policy πθ and the frozen reference policy πref each score both answers. The core quantity in DPO isβ[(log πθ(y+|x)−log πθ(y−|x))−(log πref(y+|x)−log πref(y−|x))]. π is a probability model and β is the coefficient that controls the scale of the objective; the log probability here usually comes from multiplying the conditional probabilities of the answer tokens and taking the log, which makes it the sum of the per-token log probabilities.[DPO]

So "the current model gives the good answer a higher probability" is still not enough to explain the update. In the teaching example the current pair is −2 and −4 and the reference pair is −1 and −3; the preference gap is 2 in both, the corrected margin is 0, and the loss is ln2≈0.693147. Looking only at the current gap of 2 misjudges how much has been learned.

The label mask decides what you are really optimizing

Check whether the prompt part is excluded, which token the answer starts at, whether EOS counts, whether padding counts, and whether both paths use the same template and truncation rules. If the prompt is included in the sequence scoring, its common term may cancel under some settings, but do not take that ideal cancellation as a license in code: padding, truncation and batching differences can break it, and it creates unnecessary computation.

In particular, do not freely swap mean(token logp) and sum(token logp) for each other. The former brings length normalization and is no longer the same objective; comparisons between long and short answers change. A different objective may be designed, but it has to be declared a variant and validated again, not claimed to be merely "for numerical stability".

Caching the reference probabilities needs a version identity too

The reference policy is fixed, so precomputation can reduce the training cost; but the cache has to be bound to the reference weights, the tokenizer, the template, the answer text, the truncation and the mask rules. Using only the sample ID as the key and then reading the old logp after the text has been revised brings unrelated reference probabilities into the gradient. A cache hit is not the same as a valid cache.

Follow-up: does winning on preference mean being factually correct?

No. Preference data may reward length, tone or ingratiation rather than fact. Validation should check the preference metrics, the executable task result, the refusal behavior and the length distribution together. The objective in the paper describes how to make use of pairwise preference; it offers no guarantee that "any preference label is reliable".

Follow-up: does raising β just make it learn faster?

It cannot be treated as a learning rate directly. It changes the scale at which the margin enters the logistic loss, and it also ties into what the reference constraint means as a model; what it controls is not what the optimizer's learning rate controls. When tuning it, record the log-probability gap, the length and how far things saturate, rather than watching a single training loss.

Q086｜Why do reasoning models often use reinforcement learning? How do PPO, GRPO and process supervision differ?

What to know first for this question A policy is a probability distribution over actions, a probability ratio compares how much the new and the old policy support the same action, and KL measures how far the distributions diverge; none of these quantities is the rate of correct answers.
Lesson 44 · Probability, exponentials and softmaxLesson 54 · The mathematics needed before post-training

Core answer

Start with the objects and symbols used here
PPO、GRPO、advantage
These are concepts from policy optimization. PPO commonly limits the update with a ratio of new to old action probabilities, plus clipping; GRPO-style methods build the advantage by comparing rewards within a group. An advantage is performance relative to a baseline, not the probability that an answer is correct. Independent raw-difference example: rewards [0,1,1] minus the group mean of 2/3 give [-2/3,1/3,1/3]. An algorithm may normalize them again, so the two forms must not be mixed.

When the task result can be verified, the solution trajectories the model generates can be rewarded, which encourages more effective multi-step reasoning. PPO is a family of constrained policy-update methods; GRPO builds an advantage estimate from the relative rewards of a group of candidates, which reduces the dependence on a separate value model. Process supervision judges the intermediate steps and outcome supervision judges the final result; different methods can be combined, and none of them can be taken as the uniform implementation of every reasoning model.[R1]

The logic of one round of training on a verifiable task

Take a coding problem with hidden tests: the problem is fixed, the current policy samples several programs, the isolated tests are run, and results such as a pass, a timeout or an illegal operation come back; the results are turned into rewards, and then the policy gradient for the sequence or the token is computed. Only once the model parameters have been updated is it training. Feeding an error log back to the model for another fix is feedback at inference time, not RL.

PPO often applies a clipped objective to the new-to-old policy probability ratio r_t=πθ(a_t|s_t)/πold(a_t|s_t), which limits how far a single update can go. The advantage A says how much better this action is than the baseline, not the raw reward itself. The value estimate, the KL term, the clipping range and the normalization all affect training.

GRPO compares relatively, using the mean and the standard deviation of the rewards of a group of answers to the same question; a common simplified advantage is A_i=(R_i−mean(R))/(std(R)+ε). It shows what "comparing within one question" does: it removes part of the influence of the question's difficulty and brings out the relative merit inside the group. But when the whole group gets the same reward, the signal inside the group can be very weak, and that is not a problem more training steps necessarily solve.

An outcome reward is clear and comparatively cheap, but a piece of wrong reasoning can arrive at the right answer by chance; a process reward gives finer credit assignment, yet it needs reliable judgment of the intermediate steps and easily marks an unusual but correct route as wrong. A long trajectory also brings computational cost and the risk of reward hacking.

Follow-up: does a longer stretch of thinking in the output mean the training was more successful?

Length is only intermediate behavior. The criterion for success should be whether the task's accuracy and reliability improve under the same resources. The model may question itself over and over, search ineffectively, or make a short task complicated. The reasoning budget should match the difficulty; not every question is worth sampling along several paths.

Follow-up: how does reward hacking arise?

When the reward only checks the public tests, the model may speculate on features of those tests without meeting the real requirement; when only the format is rewarded, it can produce a structurally perfect empty answer. Isolate the hidden validation, use several independent checks and watch the behavior out of distribution. The reward score itself must not be taken as the task's ground truth.

Follow-up: what is the difference between self-consistency and training?

Self-consistency samples several paths at inference time and then aggregates by agreement among the answers; the parameters usually do not change. It can reduce some random reasoning errors, but if the samples share the same wrong premise the majority vote is still wrong. More votes cost more, and they cannot turn errors correlated between models into independent evidence.

Q087｜Why does MoE have so many total parameters yet activate only some of them? What does the router actually do?

What to know first for this question An expert is an optional sub-network of parameters; the routing selects which of them are used this time. The total parameters, the activated parameters and the amount of data moved cannot be lumped together.
Lesson 45 · The smallest neural network modelLesson 61 · Low rank, quantization, expertsLesson 62 · Computing fast and fitting in memory

Core answer

Start with the objects and symbols used here
MoE, experts and the router
MoE is a model structure that contains several sub-networks; the router selects or weights a few experts for the current token's representation. An expert is usually a numeric sub-network, not a chat agent given a prompt such as "math teacher". Independent example: with 4 experts and 2 chosen per token, one pass activates only 2 of the expert paths; the parameters and routing state that the model design requires still have to be stored.

MoE replaces, in some layers, a single FFN with several expert networks, uses a router to pick a few experts to compute for each token, and then merges the results by weight. Shared parts such as attention are usually still executed. The total parameters decide storage and capacity, and the activated parameters are closer to the computation for a single token, but latency cannot be inferred from the activated parameters alone.[V3]

A look at a simplified Top-2 MoE layer

The input token's hidden vector is h∈R^d, the router computes g=hW_r, giving E expert scores. The top-2 indices a and b are chosen and their scores normalized into the weights w_a and w_b; the expert FFNs f_a(h), f_b(h) are computed separately, returning w_a f_a(h)+w_b f_b(h). A real model may have shared experts, a different normalization, load balancing or more complex routing, so one formula cannot be applied to describe every MoE.

Within one batch different tokens pick different experts. An implementation has to group the tokens first, send them to the matching experts, run the matrix multiplication and then return the outputs to their original positions. That produces dispatch/combine and possibly all-to-all communication across devices. However many experts there are, if one of them receives most of the tokens it becomes the bottleneck.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
FLOPs and FLOP/s: a count and a rate
FLOPs usually means a number of floating-point operations; FLOP/s means a rate of operations per second. Papers and vendors may count a multiply-add as one operation or two, so the basis has to be fixed. Dividing total operations by the theoretical peak is no promise of real elapsed time: movement, queueing, communication and actual utilization cost something too.

A balanced load is not a balanced meaning. Training uses auxiliary objectives, capacity limits or other mechanisms to reduce congestion, but each has a cost: dropping tokens over capacity can hurt quality, balancing too strongly can interfere with choosing the best expert, and the communication can swallow the theoretically saved FLOPs.

MoE routing results have to be followed token by token

Follow-up: are the experts "a mathematics expert, a law expert, a code expert"?

The name misleads easily. An expert is a block of parameters, the routing is formed by training, and patterns of specialization can appear without corresponding naturally to a discipline named by people. Seeing which expert a particular token went to does not license the claim that the model assigned it an interpretable professional role. Nor is an MoE expert a sub-Agent.

Follow-up: is it enough to load only the selected experts at inference time?

Later tokens will choose different experts, and which ones are needed is not necessarily known before the choice. Offloading experts can save VRAM, but it introduces transfer and prefetch problems; a low-latency service usually needs careful planning of which weights stay resident and how they are spread across devices. Few activated parameters does not mean a small model file.

Follow-up: how does the router differ from an agent's model routing?

The MoE router is a trainable computation inside the model at token granularity; agent routing usually picks one complete model, tool or skill at the granularity of a request or a sub-task. The two differ in budget, in how they are trained and in how they fail. Do not take "MoE already routes" as a reason not to dispatch agent tasks.

Q088｜How do you choose between LoRA, QLoRA, quantization and distillation? What do they actually change?

What to know first for this question Low rank expresses the update with smaller matrices; quantization reduces the number of bits each weight occupies; distillation trains a student with the teacher's signal. Fewer bytes does not necessarily mean less computation overall.
Lesson 42 · The arithmetic a model does mostLesson 53 · One training run end to endLesson 61 · Low rank, quantization, experts

Core answer

LoRA freezes most of the base parameters and learns the task's changes with a low-rank increment; QLoRA stores the base at low bit width and trains an adapter; quantization approximates weights or activations with fewer bits; distillation has a student learn the teacher's output or distribution. The purposes of the four can overlap, but they are not synonyms.[LORA][QLORA]

LoRA's sizes and gradient flow

If the original linear layer is W∈R^(d_out×d_in), addΔW=sBA, where A∈R^(r×d_in), B∈R^(d_out×r), with r far smaller than the dimensions on either side, and compute y=Wx+sB(Ax). Trainable parameters: d_out*d_in becomes r*(d_in+d_out). For example, a 4096×4096 matrix has 16,777,216 parameters, and at r=16 A+B is only 131,072, about 1/128 of that.

That the base is not updated does not mean the backward pass need not go through the base. To obtain the gradients of a downstream adapter or of an earlier layer, the chain still has to be traversed backwards, with the activations saved or recomputed. Many implementations initialize one low-rank matrix at random and set the other to zero, so that the initial increment is zero; if both were zero, the initial gradient path could degenerate.

Ordinary uniform quantization can be summarized as q=clip(round(w/s)+z), with the approximate value recovered asŵ=s(q−z). The scale and the zero point have to be stored; group- or channel-level quantization can reduce the error but adds metadata. Low bit width is a way of representing numbers; whether the compute kernel really accelerates at that width, and whether it has to dequantize to a higher precision, decides the actual speed.

Distillation can use high-quality answers generated by the teacher, and it can also have the student match soft targets. The student does not copy all the teacher's parameters; it approximates particular behavior under the constraints of its capacity and its training distribution. Validation has to include tasks that were not used for distillation; the teacher's errors are passed on as well.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
Schema and Structured Outputs
A schema is a set of structural rules about fields, types and the like; a structured-output mechanism makes it easier for the model to return data that follows those rules. Valid fields still do not mean that the target file exists, that access has been granted, or that the request is right for the business. Independent example: path is required to be a string; {"path":3} does not match the type rule, and {"path":"missing.py"} follows the structure yet may still find no file.

Follow-up: can LoRA be merged into W without loss?

In a supported ordinary linear layer and precision setting one can compute W'=W+sBA, which saves the extra branch; mathematically it is equivalent to the same linear increment, though floating point and requantization can still introduce differences. Adding several adapters together is also no guarantee that their behavior in the business will not conflict. When adapters are switched dynamically, the adapter's identity should be taken into the cache and the version management.

Follow-up: should fine-tuning or RAG come first?

Start with where the error comes from. Missing up-to-date facts call for retrieval; insufficient stability of format or decision style can be addressed with examples and fine-tuning; a tool that will not execute means changing the Harness; a great deal of irrelevant context means changing the selection. Fine-tuning without locating the root cause can burn knowledge that goes stale quickly into the parameters and still not solve the tool or data-permission problem.

Follow-up: how do you tell whether quantization has damaged real agent ability?

Do not only measure perplexity on static text: measure structured arguments, the use of evidence in a long context, code regression, tool selection and stability across turns as well. A tiny change in one logit can lead to a different branch being chosen, which is then amplified across a whole task. Run a paired evaluation under the same harness and keep a high-precision fallback path, rather than only comparing how far the VRAM went down.

Engineering deep dive · E12 · continues Q024

A zero gradient on A at LoRA's first step can be the design working normally, not a broken backward pass

Prerequisite: Lesson 52 · Lesson 61. Reading order: confirm the run contract first, then look at the counterexamples and the fix, and finally test the limits.

Fix the parameter naming first, to avoid confusing AB with BA

This section uses the row-vector convention: y=xW+xAB, with A of shape [in,r], and B of shape [r,out], leaving out the scaling constant. The reference PEFT initialization makes one side random and the other zero, so that the initial increment does not change the base model's output; column-vector implementations often write the multiplication as BA. When comparing code, confirm by the shapes and by which side is zero-initialized, not by guessing from the order of the letters.[LORA]

Why the gradients are asymmetric for the moment

Let the output gradient be G. The gradient for A has to pass through B's transpose; if B=0, that gradient is 0 at the first step. The gradient for B passes through xA, which is usually non-zero when A is non-zero. Only once B has been updated for the first time can A receive a non-zero gradient at the second step.

A local counterexample with real autograd: x=[1,2], A=[[1],[2]], B=[[0,0]], target=[1,1], sum of squared errors. xA=5, the initial increment is [0,0], A's gradient is [[0],[0]] and B's is [[-10,-10]]. This gives better judgment than changing the initialization the moment a grad of 0 is seen.

When it really is a bug

If neither side has changed after several update steps, check: whether they are in the optimizer's parameter groups, whether something was detached by accident, whether the right module was selected, whether that batch went through this path, whether the step was skipped on an AMP overflow, and whether the learning rate is 0. Not every initialization scheme has a zero B, so check the configuration before judging by what is shown in this section.

Merging, quantization and caching have to be considered together

Merging LoRA, which adds the increment back into the base weights, is one way to deploy it; quantizing and dequantizing low-precision weights, and quantizing them again, can make "merge first, then quantize" and "quantized base plus separate adapter" numerically inequivalent. Changing the adapter changes K/V, so an old prefix cache must not be reused merely because the tokens are the same. A prefix-cache design has to take in extra identity information such as the adapter.[PREFIX]

Follow-up: does freezing the base mean the base need not be stored?

It does not. The forward pass still depends on W, and computing the gradients for the increment path still needs the relevant activations. What is saved is part of the trainable parameters, the gradients and the optimizer state, not all of the model's memory. When reporting VRAM, keep the weights, the activations, the gradients, the optimizer and the cache separate.

Follow-up: if the increment's rank is small, why can it still be useful without being equal to full fine-tuning?

It limits the space in which parameter updates can be expressed, yet it may happen to cover the important directions the task needs. Whether that is enough depends on the task, the target modules, the rank and the data. Do not push "fewer parameters" into "no loss of capability"; compare with a validation set at the same budget and by the types of task failure.
