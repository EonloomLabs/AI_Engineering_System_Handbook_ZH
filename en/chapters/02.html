Prerequisites for this chapter

Every matrix expression in this chapter is made of two things: multiplying numbers by different coefficients and adding them up, and rearranging those numbers. Attention first finds the matching scores, then turns them into shares that sum to 1, and finally mixes the content according to those shares.

Each question in this chapter lists the groundwork it needs. You can take the matching Foundations lesson first, or use the self-check to confirm how well you have it.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
Query, Key, Value: three intermediate numeric representations
q is one row of query representation for the position receiving information; k is one row of matching representation offered by a readable position; v is one row of content representation that position contributes to the mix. Capital Q/K/V collect several rows into tables. They are computed from the input and the parameters, and they carry the separate jobs of scoring and of supplying content. One q dotted with two k gives two scores; softmax turns them into two shares; those shares multiply the two rows of v, and the results are added. Nothing here generates a search term, and nothing here picks an answer directly.

A quick check before the deep reading: first work out by hand the scores of q=[1,0] against the two rows of K, then compute the result with V=[[10,0],[0,20]]. Do not treat Q, K and V as three separate input texts.

A term with a dotted underline can be clicked for a quick reference. Read the definition of a letter in a formula first; do not let a guessed abbreviation stand in for the derivation.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
SwiGLU, Wg/Wu/Wd: two transformation paths, then combined
This is a gated feed-forward structure. Wg produces the path that goes through SiLU, Wu produces the other content path, the two are multiplied element by element, and Wd maps the result back to the original width. These Ws are three sets of parameters, not three agents. To read (SiLU(XWg)⊙XWu)Wd, compute XWg and XWu first, then SiLU, then ⊙, and finally multiply by Wd. Every position uses the same set of parameters independently.
Norm, LayerNorm, RMSNorm: not a probability normalization
In a layer formula, Norm stands for a normalizing function. LayerNorm usually subtracts the mean over one token's features, divides by the standard deviation, then scales and shifts; RMSNorm usually does not subtract the mean and scales by the root mean square. Neither of them is softmax. Independent micro-example, ignoring the small positive ε that stabilizes the denominator and the learnable scaling: LayerNorm([1,3])=[-1,1]; RMSNorm([1,3])=[1/√5,3/√5].
pre-norm / post-norm: where normalization goes
pre-norm normalizes first and then enters the sub-layer, with the residual added straight from the original input; post-norm adds the sub-layer result to the original input and normalizes after that. This describes the order of operations, not two phases before and after training. Independent structural comparison: x+F(Norm(x)) and Norm(x+F(x)) are not generally equal. F stands for the sub-layer function being wrapped here.

This chapter works mainly with a teaching decoder-only structure using pre-norm. The original 2017 paper was an encoder-decoder architecture; RMSNorm, RoPE and SwiGLU are later common choices; these combinations must not be described as the original paper's implementation, or as one shared by every commercial model. The shapes and numbers throughout this chapter serve to explain the algorithm; they are not an actual serving configuration.

Core answer

Start with the objects and symbols used here
Wout, LM head: the vocabulary scoring parameters
The parameter layer that turns the final hidden representation into a score for every vocabulary candidate. The input width is d, the vocabulary has Vocab entries, and the right-multiplied parameter shape is [d,Vocab]. Producing probabilities means normalizing these scores as well. Attention's Wo outputs hidden features; Wout outputs scores over vocabulary candidates. Both names contain "output", but what they output is different.
MHA, MQA, GQA: how heads are shared
An attention head is one path that matches and then aggregates content. MHA usually gives every query head its own K/V head; MQA has several query heads share one group of K/V; GQA sits between them, sharing by group. These are structural choices, not a switch to be flipped at run time. Independent shape example: 8 query heads and 2 K/V heads, grouped evenly, means every 4 query heads share 1 group of K/V; the cache is accounted for by the number of K/V heads.

For each layer, first normalize the current hidden state, then project out Q/K/V; add the position mechanism and the visibility constraint, compute attention and mix the Value; after the output projection it is added back as a residual; then come normalization and the feed-forward network, with a second residual. After every layer has been repeated there is a final normalization and a vocabulary projection, and the last position's logits are used to predict the next Token. [TR][ROPE][GQA]

Pinning down the identity of each tensor

Let B=1,T=4,d=8,H=2,Dh=4, for now using MHA. X has shape [1,4,8], 4 positions with 8 numbers each. Note: one position is one token, not necessarily one word.

In sequence Operation Shape What this step actually changes
1 U=Norm(X) [1,4,8] Controls the feature scale at each position; does not mix different positions
2 Q=U Wq,K=U Wk,V=U Wv each [1,4,8] Maps the same input into three subspaces: matching, being matched, and being read
3 Split into heads and transpose each [1,2,4,4] 2 heads, each handling 4 positions and 4 feature dimensions
4 Apply the position transform to Q/K Same as above Makes the relevance include the positional relation
5 S=QKᵀ/√Dh+M [1,2,4,4] Scores every query position against every visible key position
6 Along the last dimension, softmax [1,2,4,4] Each query's row of scores becomes read weights
7 O=A V [1,2,4,4] Weights the historical Values by each row's weights
8 Merge the heads, project with Wo, residual [1,4,8] Fuses the per-head results back into the main hidden space
9 Norm→FFN→residual [1,4,8] Applies a nonlinear feature transform at each position

The two "4"s in row 5 mean different things: one is the query length, the other the key length. In cached decoding they become 1 and the total history length, and must not be hard-coded just because they happen to be equal during training. The softmax in row 6 has to be over the key-position axis, because that decides which positions the current token reads from. A softmax along the head axis may be shape-legal, but the algorithm has already been swapped out.

Once one layer ends, its output is the next layer's input. The next layer projects again with its own Wq/Wk/Wv and does not reuse the previous layer's K/V. Adding layers does not mean repeating the same search several times; it means transforming further on a representation that has already fused the context. The final vocabulary projection is generally not each head sampling a word of its own: the whole layer's representation is fused first, and then the entire vocabulary is scored.

How to read the changes in the figure

Go down the table's second column to check whether adjacent operations can be multiplied, then read the third: many projections exchange no information across tokens.

What actually brings in information from other positions is the weights times V; matrix multiplication is only a uniform computational tool, and multiplying along different axes means different things.

Adding a residual requires the shapes on both sides to match. Widening to 8 is the FFN's intermediate width and cannot be added straight onto the 4-dimensional main path.

Do not conflate: if a [3,3] weight matrix is mistaken for 3 vocabulary candidates, the program may still run softmax, but the reading is already wrong: the columns here are input positions, not the output vocabulary.

Follow-up: why does prefill usually have to compute every input position rather than only the last one?

In layer 2 the last position has to read the layer-2 K/V of the earlier positions, and those K/V come from the hidden states those positions hold after layer 1. So one cannot take the raw Embedding and skip straight over the intermediate layers of every history position. A causal mask gives the dependencies among history positions a direction, but it does not remove the need for them as input to the later layers. An implementation may leave out intermediate logits it does not need; it cannot generally leave out the work of building the history representation.

Follow-up: the attention output has already mixed information, so why do we still need Wo?

Start with the objects and symbols used here
Concat, split and chunk: joining and splitting
Concat joins several arrays along a chosen axis; it does not add their elements. split or chunk groups them at specified boundaries. Every axis that is not being joined has to be compatible. chunk here means a block of an array, whereas a chunk in networking is a slice of bytes. Independent example: two heads of [2,3] each, joined along the feature axis, give [2,6]; joined along the position axis they would give [4,3], which already means something different.

Each head produces its result in its own subspace, and concatenation only places them side by side; Wo lets the model learn how to combine linearly across heads and map back to the residual width. Without Wo it is not mathematically impossible to build a model, but the expressive structure changes and training and evaluation have to be redone; it cannot simply be deleted from an existing model.

Q074｜Why are Q, K and V split into three? What does attention actually compute?

What to know first for this question Q is the numbers with which the current position states what it wants to match, K is the numbers of each candidate being compared, and V is the content gathered according to the shares; all three usually come from different transforms of the same input.
Lesson 42 · The arithmetic a model does mostLesson 46 · Attention step by step

Core answer

Q and K together decide "how a position takes information from the visible positions", and V decides "what the content taken is". They come from learnable projections; their meaning is not a human-fixed "question, keyword, answer". The attention output is a weighted mixture of Values — it does not generate a word directly, and it is not an exact database query.[TR]

Working through an example you can compute by hand

Let the inputs at the three positions be x1=[1,0], x2=[0,1], x3=[1,1], with a single head and, for now, no normalization and no positional rotation. Let Wq=Wk=I, Wv=[[1,2],[3,4]], then:

q3=[1,1]
k1=[1,0]  k2=[0,1]  k3=[1,1]
v1=[1,2]  v2=[3,4]  v3=[4,6]

The third position can see all three positions. The three dot products are 1、1、2, divided by √2 giving 0.7071、0.7071、1.4142. Softmax gives about 0.248255、0.248255、0.503490. So the output at the third position is:

o3 = 0.248255×[1,2] + 0.248255×[3,4] + 0.503490×[4,6]
   ≈ [3.006980, 4.510470]

The weights sum to 1 over the three positions, but the output is not any one original Value: it is a mixture. That is why the token with the largest weight cannot simply be called "the only cause". The residual, the other heads, the feed-forward that follows and the changes between layers all affect the final output as well.

If Wv is changed while Q/K are left alone, the read proportions can stay the same while the content read changes; if only Q is changed, the current query distributes the read proportions differently. Keeping Q/K/V separate lets the matching space and the content space serve different purposes. Forcing all three to share reduces the parameters and changes the inductive bias; it is not equivalent to the standard structure.

The first position sees only itself, so the weights should be [1,0,0]; the second position sees only the first two. This example also shows that the causal mask and content relevance are two separate mechanisms: a token that is highly relevant but lies in the future must still be masked out.

Follow-up: does the "self" in self-attention mean looking only at itself?

No. It means that Q and K/V come from the same sequence, or from the same source of representation; different positions in the sequence can still read one another, and the range is decided by the mask. In cross-attention Q usually comes from one sequence and K/V from another — a decoder reading an encoder's output, for example. A decoder-only chat model does not need cross-attention in every layer.

Follow-up: why not just use the raw X as Value?

Using X directly is a variant one could design, but a learnable V projection lets the model choose a content representation suited to being aggregated, and it can also change the per-head width. Existing weights depend on the structure used in training, so the V projection cannot be deleted while the same result is expected. Likewise K is not a fixed index table: the K at each position depends on that layer's contextual representation.

Follow-up: is attention simply "semantic similarity"?

It is a matching function the model has learned; it may express positional copying, syntactic dependency, pattern matching and so on, and it need not agree with human semantic similarity. The dot product is also affected by vector scale, and it is usually not a cosine retrieval over the embeddings of external documents. Calling both of them retrieval has value as an analogy, but the algorithm and the evaluation metric must not be mixed up.

Projected twice, scaled twice, still random under eval: three traps in same-named Attention interfaces

Prerequisite: Lesson 42 · Lesson 46 · Lesson 49. Reading order: confirm the run contract first, then look at the counterexamples and the fix, and finally test the limits.

First, be clear which part of the computation chain the interface sits in

Q=XWq's Q is a Query that has already been projected. A high-level MultiheadAttention interface may take the pre-projection input and do the projection inside; the low-level SDPA takes Q/K/V that have already been projected and arranged into head dimensions. Calling both interfaces "attention" does not remove this boundary. Read the interface's input shape, which parameter tables belong to it and who is responsible for the scaling, and then decide which step the caller performs.[MHA] [SDPA]

The first: a square matrix lets a transpose error pass silently

Textbooks use right-multiplication notation and write X @ W, where W has shape [in,out]; PyTorch Linear stores the parameter as [out,in]. This book takes X=[[1,2]], W=[[1,3],[2,4]]. The correct output is [5,11]; transposing once more by mistake gives [7,10]. Because W is 2×2 both can be multiplied, so a shape test catches nothing at all.

A test has to use an asymmetric and preferably non-square weight, and check the actual values. Weight conversion, splitting into heads, merging heads and similar paths should all be tested with this kind of small "runs but is not equivalent" matrix rather than with the identity matrix alone: the identity matrix hides many transpose errors.

The second: the scaling is done twice

By default SDPA internally scales the dot product by 1/√d_k. If Q has already been divided by √d_k before the call and the default SDPA is then used, the scores as a whole are shrunk to 1/d_k. That changes the softmax distribution; it is not a harmless rearrangement. If a custom scale parameter is set, the semantics of the current version have to be checked; do not handle it once in the tensor and once again in the function's options.[SDPA]

The third: model.eval() does not take over a bare function's arguments

A Module's eval mode changes the module's behavior; it does not magically turn an arbitrary function argument into 0. The PyTorch documentation states explicitly that SDPA applies dropout according to dropout_p; at inference the caller has to pass 0.[SDPA]

# 在自定义Module.forward中显式绑定训练状态

Follow-up: is fixing the seed enough?

Fixing the seed helps locate a random path, but it must not be used to cover up a dropout that was switched on by mistake. Different kernels, precisions and batch shapes can still take different numeric paths. Switch off the random operations that should not be there first, then compare within a tolerance; only when bit-for-bit reproduction is genuinely required should the device and deterministic algorithms be constrained as well.

Follow-up: why can returning the attention weights be slower?

Asking for the full weights can mean the implementation has to materialize a large intermediate that could otherwise have been skipped, and it may even change which fast paths are available. When diagnosing, record need_weights, the training state, the dtype, the mask type and the backend; do not compare function names alone. A performance conclusion has to be measured on the target hardware.

Q075｜Why divide by √d? Why softmax? Why must the maximum be subtracted for numerical stability?

What to know first for this question A dot product gives a score that can be positive or negative; scaling controls its magnitude; softmax turns the scores into weights that are non-negative and sum to 1. A weight is not confidence in a fact.
Lesson 44 · Probability, exponentials and softmaxLesson 46 · Attention step by step

Core answer

Under a common initialization approximation, the variance of a d-dimensional dot product grows with d; dividing by √d keeps the magnitude from expanding linearly with the dimension and lowers the risk of softmax saturating too early. Softmax turns the scores into weights that are non-negative and sum to 1. Subtracting the maximum leaves the mathematical distribution unchanged while markedly lowering the risk of exponential overflow.[TR]

Deriving the scaling from the variance, instead of just memorizing the conclusion

Start with the objects and symbols used here
Σ: sum term by term over the subscript
Σ is shorthand for addition. The subscript says which term varies; a subscript that is not summed over stays fixed. It is not an extra parameter, and it does not mean concatenating arrays. Independent micro-example: Σᵢ xᵢyᵢ with x=[1,2] and y=[3,4] is just 1×3+2×4=11.
Start with the objects and symbols used here
E, Π, ∼, ∝: common notation in probability expressions
x∼p means sampling from the distribution p, E means taking the expectation under the stated distribution, Π means multiplying the stated terms together, and ∝ means the two sides differ by a common factor rather than being equal already. Here E is not the embedding parameter table. Independent example: pᵢ∝exp(zᵢ) still has to be divided by the sum of the exponentials over all candidates; where ∝ is written, an unnormalized value must not be read straight off as a probability.

Let qi,ki be independent with mean 0 and variance 1, and the dimensions independent; then E[qi ki]=0, Var(qi ki)=1. The dot product S=Σi qi ki has Var(S)=d, with standard deviation √d. So S/√d has variance 1. Vectors after real training do not satisfy these conditions strictly, but this explains the design motivation for the scaling; it is not an exact estimate of the variance of each sample.

If the scores become [100,0,-100], softmax puts almost all of its mass on the first entry. The derivative of softmax contains pi(1-pi) and-pi pj, so when p is extreme many gradients become very small. A model that is too sharp this early in training learns with difficulty. The scaling is one part of stabilizing training; it does not replace normalization, initialization, the learning rate or the other factors.

For any constant c, we have exp(zi-c)/Σj exp(zj-c)=exp(zi)/Σj exp(zj). Taking c=max(z) makes every exponential at most 1, which avoids exp(1000) overflow. This is not "to make the probabilities smoother", because in exact mathematics the distribution does not change at all. It is a numerically stable implementation of the same formula.

Follow-up: why does a fully masked row so easily produce NaN?

If every score is -∞, the maximum is -∞ too, and subtracting gives -∞-(-∞), so there is no valid normalized distribution. A correct implementation avoids such a query in advance, or settles on behavior such as returning zero or skipping padding positions. Do not expect "use a stable softmax" to resolve an undefined input by itself. The kernel's own convention has to agree with the mask logic above it.

Follow-up: why not just use argmax to take one Value?

A hard selection changes the model's expressiveness and the nature of its gradients; softmax provides a differentiable soft mixture, letting several positions contribute together and letting the matching parameters be updated through the loss. Both sharp and spread-out weights after training can be useful. Some sparse or routing models do use a different selection mechanism, but that has to be designed separately; it cannot simply be substituted for the attention of a network that has already been trained.

Follow-up: is the softmax in attention the same thing as the softmax at generation time?

It is the same kind of mathematical normalization, over a different axis and with a different meaning. Attention normalizes over the "visible key positions" to give weights for reading information; the output softmax normalizes over the "vocabulary tokens" to give the probability of the next token. The first does not directly decide which word is output; only the second takes part in decoding and sampling.

When a whole row is masked out, why does subtracting the maximum still not save softmax?

Prerequisite: Lesson 44 · Lesson 47. Reading order: confirm the run contract first, then look at the counterexamples and the fix, and finally test the limits.

Numerical stability has a precondition that often goes unwritten

Subtracting the maximum eases exponential overflow, but only if the row holds at least one finite score that may take part. If every candidate is forbidden, the correct reading is not "choose one of these candidates" but this row has no legal distribution. Visibility has to be dealt with before any numeric trick.

This book uses two forbidden positions. If the two cells are filled with the finite value −1e9, softmax returns [0.5,0.5]; there is no NaN, but the forbidden content has been averaged in. If they are filled with −∞, a hand-written "subtract the maximum" produces −∞−(−∞), which gives NaN. The two spellings fail in different ways, so "no NaN" cannot be used as proof of correctness.

A library's special handling is not a change in the mathematical definition

In this run, PyTorch 2.10 CPU SDPA was observed to output 0 for an all-False boolean mask, while a hand-written softmax on all negative infinities gave NaN. This local measurement establishes only the behavior of that version and that backend; it does not mean that every fused kernel and every dtype behaves the same way. The official documentation likewise asks readers to watch for behavioral and floating-point differences between implementations.[SDPA]

Which fix to choose depends on what the row is

If it is a PAD query, its output should generally not be used as a valid token at all: it can be explicitly zeroed or ignored later, and it must be kept out of the loss and out of any pooling denominator. If it is a real query with no readable Key, that is usually a mask, position-offset or indexing error, and the error should be surfaced rather than turned into a uniform distribution. If it is a generation-side constraint that forbids the whole vocabulary, an explicit failure or relaxation policy is needed; a forbidden item must not be sampled quietly.

First compute has_valid = allowed.any(-1), then branch according to the task's semantics. Once a safe placeholder has been used so that an invalid row can take part in the arithmetic, the placeholder's result must still be excluded from the business result. Using only nan_to_num to wipe NaN away makes an upstream error hard to find.

Acceptance should cover four boundary cases

A normal row with several candidates, a row with only one candidate, a fully forbidden PAD row and a fully forbidden real row must each have their own assertion. Checking that "the weight of a disallowed position is 0", that "the weights of a valid row sum to 1" and that "an invalid row is not counted in the loss" discriminates better than a blanket check that the output is finite. Mixed precision has to be tested as well, because a very large negative number that is finite in one dtype may overflow to negative infinity in another.

Follow-up: with only one legal Key, is it a bug that the output does not change when Q/K do?

Not necessarily. With a single candidate the softmax is always 1 and the output equals that Value; a change in Q/K has no competitor to act on. Attention should be tested with at least two visible positions that have different scores and different Values; a single-Key case is no evidence that the gradient path already works.

Follow-up: if PAD's embedding is set to 0, is a mask no longer needed?

It does not hold. A zero vector can become a non-zero representation after a bias, position handling, normalization or another layer; and even with Value at 0, PAD can still take a share of the softmax and change the normalization of the valid content. Filling the content with 0 and forbidding participation are therefore two different constraints.

Q076｜Why does a layer need residuals, Norm and an FFN as well as attention?

What to know first for this question The residual adds the original input back, normalization controls the scale of each row of numbers, and the feed-forward layer applies a transform with a nonlinearity at each position; they do not do quite the same job.
Lesson 45 · The smallest neural network modelLesson 49 · Fitting attention into one layer

Core answer

Attention handles the exchange of information between positions, the FFN handles the position-by-position nonlinear transform, the residual provides a direct path for information and gradients, and Norm controls the feature scale. They solve different problems: the FFN is not an optional decoration, and the residual is not a mechanism that guarantees training will never fail.[TR][V3]

Writing one pre-norm layer as two equations

U = X + Attention(Norm1(X))
Y = U + FFN(Norm2(U))
Start with the objects and symbols used here
∂, ∇: partial derivative and gradient
∂L/∂w asks how the loss L changes when w changes a little while the other independent variables are held fixed. These rates of change across many parameters make up the gradient ∇L. They are derivative notation, not two ordinary numbers divided by each other. Independent micro-example: for L=w², the gradient at w=3 is 6. In the simplest update w←w−η×6, η is the learning rate.
Jacobian: the table of derivatives for several outputs
When a function has several inputs and outputs, the Jacobian records in one table the partial derivative of every output with respect to every input. J_F denotes that table for the function F; it is not a new matrix in the training data. Independent micro-example: F([x₁,x₂])=[2x₁,3x₂], J_F=[[2,0],[0,3]]. The derivative table of the residual x+F(x) is I+J_F.

In this structure the X on the residual main path is not replaced outright by the attention output: what the model learns is what to add to the representation it already has. On the backward pass, ∂U/∂X contains the identity term I, so the gradient can travel back along a more direct path; this eases the difficulty of optimizing deep networks, but the overall gradient is still affected by each branch's Jacobian, by the training configuration and by the numeric state.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
BatchNorm: statistics across samples, unlike LayerNorm
BatchNorm usually computes its statistics for one feature or channel along the batch axis and the applicable spatial axes; LayerNorm computes along the feature dimensions designated inside one sample. BatchNorm may use statistics from different sources in training and in evaluation, and the details still depend on the configuration. The word Norm alone is no reason to mix up the statistics axes of different normalization layers; nor should two independent users come to share one session context because of it.

LayerNorm subtracts the mean from the features at each position, then divides by the standard deviation and applies a learnable scale and shift. RMSNorm usually does not subtract the mean: it scales by the root mean square and applies a learnable weight. They do not take statistics across the whole batch; so the Norm here is not the BatchNorm common in image networks. Pre-norm normalizes first and then enters the sub-layer, while post-norm normalizes after the residual addition, and the optimization behaves differently.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
⊙: element-wise multiplication, not matrix multiplication
Elementwise multiplication multiplies the numbers at the same position once the shapes are aligned; it does not then sum along any dimension. Matrices written side by side in a formula, or @, usually mean matrix multiplication, and the two are not interchangeable. Independent micro-example: [1,2]⊙[3,4]=[3,8], whereas the dot product is 11. The ⊙ in a gated feed-forward network modulates one path with the other, feature by feature.
SiLU: the input times a gating coefficient
SiLU(x)=x×sigmoid(x). The input is first mapped to between 0 and 1, then multiplied back by x itself, which makes it nonlinear. Negative numbers do not all become 0 as they do with ReLU. Independent micro-example: SiLU(0)=0; SiLU(1) is about 0.731059; SiLU(-1) is about -0.268941.
Start with the objects and symbols used here
σ: tell the function symbol from the statistic
In σ(XW+b), σ usually stands for whatever nonlinear function this layer has chosen; it may be ReLU or another, and σ alone does not make it a sigmoid. In statistical formulas σ usually means the standard deviation and σ² the variance. They are not the same object. Independent example: if σ is specified as ReLU, σ(-1)=0; if it is specified as sigmoid, the value is about 0.268941. The function has to be defined before any result can be computed.

An ordinary two-layer FFN can be written σ(XW1+b1)W2+b2: widen first, then the nonlinearity, then back down to d. The SwiGLU form can be written (SiLU(XWg) ⊙ XWu)Wd, where the multiplicative gate controls which features pass. It uses the same set of weights at every position but does not read other positions directly; information from other positions enters this position's representation through attention first, and is only then processed by the FFN.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
MoE, experts and the router
MoE is a model structure that contains several sub-networks; the router selects or weights a few experts for the current token's representation. An expert is usually a numeric sub-network, not a chat agent given a prompt such as "math teacher". Independent example: with 4 experts and 2 chosen per token, one pass activates only 2 of the expert paths; the parameters and routing state that the model design requires still have to be stored.

The parameter count also shows that the FFN is no small accessory. Ignoring bias, MHA's four d×d projections come to about 4d² parameters; if an ordinary FFN has an inner dimension of 4d, its two projections come to about 8d². If SwiGLU has an inner dimension m, its three matrices come to about 3dm. A particular modern model may use a different m, GQA or MoE, but "attention must take the most parameters and compute" cannot be treated as a general rule.

Normalization controls numeric scale: [-1,1] allows negative numbers and sums to 0. It must not be confused with softmax, which is non-negative and sums to 1.

Follow-up: is the FFN simply a database that stores facts?

Some research reads part of the feed-forward behavior as a key-value association, but not all knowledge can be located precisely as individual database records. Reproducing a fact depends on the embedding, attention, the feed-forward network, the paths between layers and the context acting together. Treating one neuron as the sole switch for a fact oversimplifies a distributed representation.

Follow-up: does more Norm mean more stability?

Adding it at will is not an improvement. A new normalization changes the scale of the information, the residual structure and the training distribution, and it can break the behavior of existing weights. For a trained model, use the original architecture first; studying a variant calls for training and evaluation. In engineering, too, locate where the NaN appears before anything else, rather than adding Norm everywhere the moment a NaN is seen.

Follow-up: what does a broken layer usually look like?

A wrong softmax axis may raise nothing while distorting the output; a residual wrongly turned into an overwrite changes the network; a transposed FFN dimension may raise a shape error outright; a low-precision overflow may propagate NaN. Start debugging from single-operator shapes, finite values and a fixed-input comparison, then compare the whole layer and the whole model. A falling training loss shows only that some objective is being optimized; it does not prove that the implementation matches the intended architecture.

RoPE: rotating inside Q/K, and why that can express relative position

An independent two-dimensional comparison with the vectors q=k=[1,0]. After position m is rotated by the angle θm and position n by θn, the dot product of the two vectors is cos(θm−θn), and that difference depends on the relative position. In general each pair of features uses a different frequency, so rotating a whole vector by one angle must not be taken for real multi-frequency RoPE.

R(θ)[x₀,x₁] = [x₀ cosθ − x₁ sinθ, x₀ sinθ + x₁ cosθ]

R preserves length; once matching rotations have been applied to Q and K, the match is affected by the difference in position. The worked model, by contrast, adds P[pos] before the network is entered, and the two position mechanisms must not be written together as one step.

The cache normally holds K that has already been processed at its original position; when generation continues, the new Q/K are processed at their new absolute positions.
