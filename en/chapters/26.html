Prerequisites for this chapter

An application blueprint is not a platter of technical terms but "which input is allowed, what evidence to look for, which actions to take, and how the result is proved". The same basic framework carries different risks in Q&A, in coding and in a GUI.

Each question in this chapter lists the groundwork it needs. You can take the matching Foundations lesson first, or use the self-check to confirm how well you have it.

Q105｜How can enterprise knowledge Q&A not only answer, but also prove that the answer applies and is not out of date?

What to know first for this question Enterprise Q&A confirms identity and evidence first, then answers with citations; expired knowledge and insufficient evidence each need a defined behavior rather than forced generation.
Lesson 22 · Compaction is not magicLesson 17 · From search results to a trustworthy answerLesson 23 · Foundations of long-term memoryLesson 05 · The denominator of an evaluation metricLesson 29 · Security basics

Core answer

Start with the objects and symbols used here
DPR, DPI and coordinate units
DPR usually means the ratio of device pixels to CSS pixels; DPI means dots per inch. Screenshot pixels, the interface's logical coordinates and screen coordinates need not share a unit, and there may also be scaling, cropping and offsets. In an independent example where DPR=2 is stated and the other mappings agree, a CSS x-coordinate of 100 corresponds to 200 device pixels; this conditional relationship cannot be carried straight over to every screenshot.

Break the Q&A into settling the identity and time range, retrieving evidence, checking applicability, generating claims, verifying citations, and replying. Retrieval similarity is only responsible for finding candidates; the version, the permissions and the conditions decide whether a candidate can serve as grounds. Where necessary, admit that the evidence is not enough rather than inventing a policy.[DPR][CTXRETRIEVAL]

One run of "can I claim this expense?"

When the question arrives, settle the user's organization and the date of the expense first, so that a historical claim is not answered with the current policy. The retrieval entry point filters the readable documents by identity, then narrows or widens the candidates by the date the policy took effect. Parsing should keep the main clause, the exceptions, the amount thresholds and the relationship to attachments; it must not return only the sentence "claimable".

Suppose the candidates include the ordinary travel policy, the exception for overseas travel, and one expired version. The system should represent them as evidence carrying a source and a validity interval, rather than stitching them into unordered paragraphs. Before generating, list the claims that have to be answered: whether the expense type is covered, the amount limit, and whether approval is needed. Every claim must have applicable evidence; if the region of the expense is missing, say that this condition is not yet settled rather than assuming the ordinary rule applies.

Finally, check that the citation really points at text that supports the claim. A citation existing is not the same as support: a paragraph about transport costs cannot prove a lodging cap. In the reply, keep "the explicit wording of the policy", "what follows from the conditions given" and "material still to be confirmed" apart. Where a real reimbursement decision is involved, the organization's authorization still has to be followed; an answering tool should not acquire financial approval authority on its own.

A correct answer in enterprise Q&A has to pass three gates at once: scope, time and evidence

How to read the changes in the figure

This system is not a case of "the more documents, the better the answer". Without an identity and a scope of use, even the most relevant number can be wrong for the current user.

Being allowed to read a rule does not mean the rule applies to the person at hand; authorization filtering and judging the business scope are two separate checks.

When a necessary condition cannot be verified, state the gap or ask, instead of stitching numbers together out of several candidates.

Do not conflate: the amounts in the figure are purely teaching numbers; facts, policy and permissions must all come from the real system rather than being filled in by the model.

Worked example · Q105

How enterprise Q&A connects every clause of the answer back to evidence

Input and conditions. The question: "a trial user applies on a Saturday for a refund on day 6 — is that allowed?" The fictional policy says "within 7 days and accepted on working days only".

Join the steps up. A trustworthy answer needs a combination of conditions and a boundary on the evidence, not just a relevant document plus a fluent retelling.

Limits and counterexamples. The policy is entirely fictional, used to explain the logic; it corresponds to no real refund or legal right.

Basis and scope of use:[DERIVE]. The example input and setting are stated explicitly by this book.

Follow-up: why not put the whole set of rules into a large context?

A small, single set of rules can simply be read in full, and does not necessarily need RAG. But when the documents are many, the permissions differ and updates are frequent, putting everything in the context adds cost and the burden of resolving conflicts. The choice should rest on the scale, the update rate and task performance, not on a default vector store or a default very long context.

Follow-up: what if the user demands "just give me a definite answer"?

Certainty comes from conditions and evidence, not from a firm tone. You can state plainly what can be confirmed under the known conditions, and point out which missing condition would change the conclusion. A user's preference for definite phrasing is no reason to ignore a contradiction in the evidence or to fill an unknown field with a favorable value.

Follow-up: why can the key of a Q&A cache not be the question text alone?

The same question may come from users with different permissions, from different organizations and on different dates; the knowledge version changes too. The cache has to be bound to the identity scope, the evidence version and the applicable conditions it needs, or else use a safe shared scope. Otherwise the cache bypasses the permission control in the retrieval stage.

Engineering deep dive · E51 · continues Q101

What is still missing between "the material says a refund is possible" and "the refund can be carried out now"?

Prerequisite: Lesson 17 · Lesson 26 · Lesson 29. Reading order: confirm the run contract first, then the counterexamples and the fix, and test the limits last.

The advisory layer and the action layer must have different evidence requirements

Knowledge-base Q&A can explain the conditions from one policy whose version is recorded; performing a write also has to check the current user, the current order, the current policy, the remaining allowance and whether it is a duplicate. This section uses refunds as the teaching business; it connects to no real payment system and provides no actual financial operation.

MCP security design requires resources and permissions to match, idempotent API practice requires the intent of a repeated call to be explicit, and agent evaluation requires looking at the result in a real environment rather than a statement in text.[MCPSEC] [IDEMP] [EVAL] The engineering inference from putting the three together is: a retrieval conclusion cannot cross a business transaction boundary directly.

Right at planning time, possibly already void at execution time

A teaching scenario: at planning time policy version 7 allows the operation; before execution the policy becomes 8 and that order no longer meets the conditions. Using the "allowed" the model remembered produces a stale write. The local test rejects the submission on the version mismatch. The point of the rejection is not that the model answered wrongly, but that the premise of the action has changed.

An action tool can accept a structured business intent, with the server re-reading the current state and submitting conditionally; do not let a "conditions met = true" supplied by the model stand in for the check. If the executor finds the state has changed, it should return an explainable conflict so the agent verifies again, rather than quietly altering the arguments to get around it.

The audit chain has to connect the explanation to the operation

Record the user's request, the policy evidence used, the actual version at execution time, the object that was approved, the intent key of the operation, the downstream result and the final reconciliation. Keep stored sensitive fields to a minimum. The success message the user sees must come from the real submission result, not from the earlier eligibility analysis text.

Follow-up: is re-reading the material before every operation wasteful?

Static descriptions and a verified cache can be reused, but dynamic eligibility, authorization and the target version must come with a guarantee of validity. Cutting down repeated queries with a version condition, a TTL or a server-side transaction is an optimization; leaving out the verification changes the security semantics. The two are not the same.

Follow-up: when a business tool returns a failure, may the agent try another tool to carry out the same action?

Judge the kind of failure first. A permission or policy refusal should not be treated as a technical error inviting "try another route"; an unknown network outcome should go down the verify-or-idempotent path; insufficient input can be supplemented with more material. Tool orchestration has to respect the boundary of the business intent; the number of actions completed is not the goal.

Q106｜What is the minimum set of internal objects a repository repair agent needs, from taking the ticket to delivery?

What to know first for this question A coding agent needs the current workspace, reliable location of the fault, bounded changes and real tests; retelling the repository's documentation is not the same as finishing the development.
Lesson 18 · Code is not an ordinary documentLesson 26 · Retries, idempotence and transactionsLesson 04 · Foundations of engineering qualityLesson 05 · The denominator of an evaluation metric

Core answer

At a minimum it has to be able to express the task's goal and constraints, a snapshot of the current code, the candidate explanations and their evidence, the change set, the execution results and the acceptance status. Files plus a few structured records are enough to start with; no complex platform has to be built right away. What matters is that these objects are clearly related to one another, instead of all being mixed into the chat history.[HARNESS][SWEBENCH]

Take the exclude path bug as the example

When the ticket is taken, record "match on paths relative to the project root; do not change the existing include rules; keep unexcluded files after the fix". Once repository version r42 has been fetched, run the smallest failing test first and save the command, the environment, the return code and the failing assertion. Then follow the call chain to check whether the path is already normalized before it enters the matching function from the caller.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
Patch, image channels and the visual projection
A patch in an image is a small block cut out of it, and RGB is the 3 channels red, green and blue; a patch in software engineering is a code patch — the same word for both. An image block, once flattened, can become a vector through a learnable transformation; it is not a lookup in a text vocabulary. Independent image example: one 16×16×3 block holds 768 numbers, and after projection it can become d features. d is fixed by the model design.

Once a candidate root cause is found, the agent should be able to answer "which piece of evidence ruled out the other explanations". For instance, only the fact that the matching library itself works correctly on relative paths while the caller passes an absolute path supports fixing the normalization at the entry point. The change becomes a standalone patch, and when it is applied the old content or version is checked; if the user has changed the file in the meantime, read it again and reconcile rather than overwrite.

Verification runs the tests tied to the root cause first, then adds regression tests according to the blast radius. The final delivery is bound to the current workspace version: what was changed, why it is right, what the tests actually ran, and what was left unverified. The model may explain this evidence, but an explanation cannot stand in for a run result. Whether to commit, push or deploy is decided by the scope of authorization, not widened automatically by the words "task complete".

No. It needs a sufficient understanding of the invariants, the callers and the verification scope that the change touches. Q107｜How does a research agent handle "several sources that contradict one another", rather than writing a fluent collage?

What to know first for this question A long research task needs persistent evidence, hypotheses and items that expire; a stage summary is for handover and does not replace the original source.
Lesson 02 · State, events and checkpointsLesson 22 · Compaction is not magicLesson 17 · From search results to a trustworthy answerLesson 23 · Foundations of long-term memoryLesson 05 · The denominator of an evaluation metric

Core answer

Organize the research around verifiable claims as its unit, recording the date of each source, the scope it applies to and what supports what. A conflict may come from dates, definitions, samples, method or a factual error; which premise the conflict sits in has to be located, rather than settled by a simple majority vote or an average.[AGENTS][MULTI]

The intermediate states from question to conclusion

Break the question into sub-questions that need evidence first, such as "what is the mechanism", "under which conditions does it work" and "what are the costs and counterexamples". A search result only enters the candidate set; an evidence record is formed only after the text itself has been read. If source A says context compaction reduces cost while B says compaction reduces reliability, that is not necessarily a real contradiction: the first measures the bill and the second measures information loss, and both may hold at once.

For a real disagreement about the same metric, compare the experimental tasks, the model versions, the budget and the definition of success. Leave a disagreement that cannot be removed in the conclusion, and say how it bears on the decision. A provisional guess made during the research should not be promoted to a long-term rule on its own; only with explicit evidence, scope and version does something enter reusable knowledge.

Before output, run a "claim—evidence" check back the other way: where does each important number, capability and causal conclusion come from? Does the original text state only a correlation rather than causation? Has a result the author reported been written up as an independent replication? Has the behavior of an old version been written up as a guarantee of the current product? These checks matter more than simply having the model "polish it to sound more professional".

Follow-up: does a conclusion get more reliable the more sources there are?

Several web pages may be copying the same news item or technical report, which is not independent evidence. Identify the chain of dependence between sources, and look first for the original data, the specification and the experiment. The number of sources can describe coverage only; it does not turn directly into confidence.

Follow-up: when is parallel research by several agents worth it?

When the sub-questions can be searched relatively independently and the cost of integration is less than the gain from parallelism. For instance, one looks into the model mechanism and another into engineering failures, and at the end they are merged along the shared structure of the question. If every agent searches the same material over again, the extra calls only magnify the redundancy.

Follow-up: when does the research end?

It ends when the key claims have enough support, the main counterexamples have been checked, the remaining uncertainty does not change the current decision, or the budget reaches its limit. Ending is not a claim to have exhausted the world's literature; mark the limits of the evidence and the new information that would change the conclusion.

Engineering mechanisms and failure boundaries

How does a research agent avoid "searched a great deal, concluded nothing"?

Start with a ledger of claims

The teaching question "is the new routing worth shipping" splits into at least six kinds of claim: recall of the skills actually needed, wrong loading, task success, time taken, cost and regressions. Each records its supporting evidence, counterexamples, the situations it applies to, and the questions left open. A search result may put forward a hypothesis, but it has not thereby confirmed a causal relationship.

Which uncertainty each round of research should reduce

The first round locates the tasks that really fail; the second follows the trajectory to see whether the rule was loaded; the third constructs a fix that holds the other conditions constant; the fourth runs the paired tasks and the counterexamples. If searching on only turns up the same material again, converge or change the method of verification rather than adding ten more relevant links.

How the sub-studies are merged

Separate agents can study tool exposure, context trimming and permissions one each, but the aggregation has to check that they share a version and a timeline. Three agents all citing the same article do not make three independent pieces of evidence. Conflicting conclusions should go back to the original request, the logs or a test, not be settled by a vote.[A04]

Stopping conditions

Stop when the evidence is sufficient for the task at hand, when an unrecoverable gap is found, when the budget is reached, or when a user decision is needed. A conditional conclusion is allowed: "on this set of tasks and versions, wrong loading went down, but cross-language repositories are not yet covered". That is more professional than an unfounded "all routing problems completely solved".

How a knowledge graph serves the main text

A node in the graph is a concrete object or a checkable claim; an edge is labeled with the relationship — calls, depends on, supports, contradicts or contrasts with — and with its source. Do not wire every related noun together into a web no one can read. The main text shows only the local subgraph the mechanism at hand needs, so that a reader can follow an edge back to the code or the record; the full graph is for checking coverage and conflicts.

A great deal of material is not the same as independent evidence

One original article retold by three blogs should still be grouped under the same root evidence in the source graph. Splitting one piece of material among several agents and then aggregating does not produce independent confirmation either. The teaching research record is divided into the claim, the original source, the conditions it applies under, the counter-evidence and the current confidence state.

The articles on multi-agent research stress the delegated goal, the artifacts and traceability; on top of that, this book recommends checking the dependence between sources when merging, rather than merging the summaries alone. Where first-hand grounds cannot be found, mark the gap; it cannot be filled with a longer explanation. The scope verified in the current version is listed in the evidence index, and what lies outside it still must not be written up as "read".[A04][A07]

Q108｜Why do long tasks and computer operation need "observe—act—verify" instead of just playing a script through in order?

What to know first for this question GUI automation observes the interface first, then locates the action, then verifies the change in the interface or the data; where an API is available it is usually more precise, but the choice has to follow the task.
Lesson 07 · APIs and streaming protocolsLesson 02 · State, events and checkpointsLesson 26 · Retries, idempotence and transactionsLesson 29 · Security basicsLesson 64 · Multimodality and trustworthiness

Core answer

A real environment changes, and an action may time out, partly succeed, or be affected by the state of the interface. The agent has to observe the current state, perform a controlled action, and then verify the effect; a task that continues across sessions also has to save recoverable progress and the external results, not only a conversation summary.[LONGHARNESS][OSWORLD]

From API first to the interface interaction you cannot avoid

Start with the objects and symbols used here
IDE, CLI, UI and DOM
IDE is the integrated development environment, CLI the command-line interface, UI the user interface, and DOM the tree of structural objects of a web document. The code, selection and page information that each entry point attaches automatically can differ. The model seeing the selected function in the IDE does not mean the same task in the CLI has received that function; check this turn's input objects first.

If the system offers a semantically clear, queryable API, prefer it: idempotency, errors and state are easier to handle that way. When the GUI must be operated, the observation comes from a screenshot, the DOM or the accessibility tree; every such observation has a time of capture, and the page may already have changed before the click. A coordinate points at the visual position at that moment, not at a permanent business object.

For example, the network is slow after "Submit" is clicked and the button does not change at once. Clicking again may submit twice. The right procedure is to wait for an observable state, query the task record, or recognize the confirmation page, rather than treating an unseen change as a failure. When it cannot be confirmed, record unknown and stop the dangerous retry. After a long task resumes, read the current page and the business state first; clicking on straight from "step 17" is not allowed.

The progress record should include the completion conditions, the confirmed results, the current environment version, the operations still to be verified, and the evidence the next step needs. Each session handover compacts only the analysis that can be compacted; it must not erase an unconfirmed side effect. This brings the GUI agent, the coding agent and business automation under one and the same principle of reliable execution.

Follow-up: the model read the screenshot correctly, so why can the operation still fail?

Recognition is only the observation stage. Scrolling, zoom, an overlay, focus, load timing and permissions all affect execution. Observe after the action, instead of letting the confidence of visual recognition stand in for business success.

Follow-up: should every task be given a full database workflow?

For a short read-only task it is usually not worth it. Persistent state becomes more necessary for tasks that cross processes, run long, have significant side effects or require human approval. Start the system simple, and let the real need for recovery decide the complexity.

First establish which coordinate space the tool uses

Screenshot pixel coordinates, CSS viewport coordinates and desktop screen coordinates may all differ. DPR describes the ratio of device pixels to CSS pixels; a screenshot may also be scaled again, cropped or given a border by the tool. The Computer Use guide requires operating by the coordinate conventions of the actual screenshot and environment.[GUI]

Pinned teaching data: a screenshot of 1600×1200 and a CSS viewport of 800×600, so the point (1000,600) in the image corresponds to CSS (500,300). Clicking (1000,600) directly overshoots the right position. This linear transform holds only on the assumption that the whole screenshot scales one-to-one with the viewport and there is no cropping offset; it must not be forced onto every desktop tool.

Correct coordinates can still click an old world

After the model has finished reading the screenshot, a dialog appears on the page, a list scrolls or the layout finishes loading, and the original coordinates now land on a different button. This is a change of state between observing and acting, not a mistake in visual recognition. Before a high-risk action, observe again or locate a structured element and check its text, its extent and its visibility.

Where a stable API or DOM tool exists, prefer its verifiable semantics; when only the screen is available, check the result after each step. Replaying an earlier sequence of coordinates cannot replace confirming the current state.

The execution feedback has to match the intent

Seeing a notice after "Save" is clicked does not always mean the target record was updated; what was saved may be another record, or only a local draft. Verify the identity of the target, the actual data and the version; keep the confirmation that an irreversible action needs. Malicious text inside an image must not turn into a high-privilege instruction either.

The button may be disabled or covered, the network may fail, or the click may land on old coordinates. Visual recognition proves where you think the button is; only feedback from the environment says what the action actually did. The two are different kinds of evidence.

Engineering mechanisms and failure boundaries

Optimization locates the bottleneck first; it is not about chasing fewer tokens

Cut the same thousand tokens, and the result can be the opposite

Removing a large repeated log may leave all the useful evidence intact; removing ten lines of key constraints may cause a wrong action. The compaction ratio is not an independent quality metric. First establish a baseline of success rate, regressions, time taken and cost on a set of real tasks, then make a paired comparison of each single change.

Three token-saving routes have to be kept apart

Tool discovery reduces the default tool descriptions; code execution leaves the bulk of the intermediate data in the execution environment and returns only the results that are needed; context selection reduces the evidence irrelevant to this turn. All of these change how information is supplied, but none amounts to stronger model parameters, and none guarantees that it suits every task.[A02][A09][A10]

When a small model turns out to be more expensive

For teaching: the strong model costs 3 per call with a success rate of 0.9, and the small model costs 1 per call with a success rate of 0.4. Estimating the cost of attempts under the ideal assumption of independent repetition does not cover the rework a failure causes, tool side effects or human review. In practice, compare complete strategies — including escalating to the strong model, extra retrieval and the cost of recovery — rather than only the price of a single call.

Cache gains and changes in the input

A stable prefix helps computation be reused, but putting a dynamic timestamp, or tools that are reordered every turn, at the front may destroy that reuse. The exact billing and caching policy depends on the vendor and the version; "the input token count did not change" does not let you infer the same cost, and a cache hit must not be read as long-term memory in the model.

Is one review fewer a step backwards

An extra review is worth its cost only when it really catches an important error. For a simple task, changing the code directly and running the relevant verification may be better; for a database migration, a permission change or complex concurrency, an independent review and failure testing are worth more. A best practice should answer "what specific failure appears if this is not done", rather than forcing the longest process onto every task.

Why one old best practice has gone back to "try it when it is needed"

The "think tool" article was published on 2025-03-20, and on 2025-12-15 the author added a note: in most cases the recommendation is now the extended thinking that has since improved. That makes a standalone think tool a historical design option rather than a component to install on every agent by default. Longer reasoning does not add evidence on its own; a delivery should still cite tool results and tests.[A17]

Tool search, programmatic tool calling and call examples address, respectively, too many tool definitions, the moving of intermediate data, and unclear argument conventions. When the problem is a wrong argument, adding tool search will not necessarily help; when the tool's network is slow, cutting the tokens the model sees will not necessarily remove the wait. This is a diagnostic derivation from the three kinds of mechanism, not a claim that the articles gave one single best recipe.[A09][A10]
