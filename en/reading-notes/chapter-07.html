Supplying information each turn

Context engineering concerns the entire visible set: rules, goals, state, evidence, tool definitions and necessary history. Prompt engineering is the part that expresses instructions. Retrieval, progressive disclosure, compaction, notes and sub-agents can all change what is visible this turn.[S084]

C(t) = 必要指令 + 任务目标 + 有效工作状态
     + 下一步需要的证据 + 工具信息 + 必要历史

This is a teaching analysis formula, not an actual serialization format. The goal is sufficient, relevant information, not a mechanically maximal or minimal amount. The entire old repository may contain outdated implementations, while supplying only one new function may lose compatibility constraints.

The nominal window and the effective capability

Lost in the Middle studies position effects; LongBench and RULER examine long-context capability across multiple tasks. Accepting a given length does not mean every task can be completed reliably; retrieving a needle also differs from combining constraints across several locations. Conclusions depend on the model, task and distracting content.[S085][S087][S088]

An original experiment places the same compatibility condition in different positions, gradually adds irrelevant logs, randomizes layouts and repeats runs. Check whether the actual patch preserves old behavior instead of only asking the model "Do you remember?"

Five content-management operations

Selection leaves irrelevant content unloaded; trimming deletes duplicate or old output; summarization re-expresses it; offloading saves original material with references; retrieval again brings it back when needed. Caching reuses computation and is not content compaction.[S084][S092][S094]

Selection may miss evidence, trimming may remove future clues, summarization may misrepresent certainty, offloading depends on valid references, and retrieving again needs sufficient clues and permissions. A common practical combination is compact task state plus externally stored original evidence, instead of only a summary that cannot be traced back.

What compaction actually keeps

Products do not share one compaction algorithm. OpenAI interfaces can return dedicated compaction state that is not meant for people to read directly; Claude offers mechanisms such as summaries, thresholds and context editing. Use the official returned structure; do not fabricate or arbitrarily split opaque state.[S089][S090][S091][S093]

Compaction can be understood as selecting candidate history, extracting state, generating a shorter representation and continuing calls. Seeing a summary does not verify every internal state, and an invisible internal process does not imply losslessness. A task summary far shorter than the original usually cannot guarantee answers to arbitrary future detail questions.

Work that needs auditing should also save original events, file versions and logs separately. Compaction is more like a lossy handoff than lossless restoration from a ZIP file. A dedicated representation should not be claimed to recover all original content either.

This is an original handoff example. Negative conclusions, prohibitions and uncertainty are the easiest to distort. Turning "May be related to Windows" into "Windows is the root cause" misleads later work. Keyword matching alone cannot detect this error.

Before and after compaction, ask: What must not change? What has been ruled out? Which tests ran? What remains a hypothesis? Then continue the actual task. Information recovery and task success must both be evaluated; even a complete summary has costs if it makes the agent repeat work.

Trade-offs among caching, compaction and resets

Compaction may change the shareable prefix and affect caching, but the extent depends on service rules and where the change occurs; it cannot simply be said to invalidate everything or necessarily save money. Comparisons should include compaction calls, later input savings, rereading evidence and rework caused by errors.[S092][S039]

Resetting context may remove old assumptions but also introduces handoff losses. Long-task research has used different combinations and adjusted them as model capabilities change; scheduled resets must not be treated as an eternal rule.[S130][S131]

Engineering mechanisms and failure boundaries

Checking the generation number when a candidate is installed

Summary S is based on the prefix e1—e3; e4 and e5 are appended while it is being generated. Appending can keep the suffix, whereas a rollback or a change of model or authorization identity invalidates the candidate.

Key boundary: a background request that is discarded can still cost money; both state correctness and usage accounting have to cover the failure path.

Follow it further through the source or the engineering basis: a verified merge is needed between the compaction candidate and the live history. Do not check only the path that goes well; check the behavior on cancellation, on a version change, and when the result is unknown.

For the complete mechanism and the continuous example see A tool result arrives while the summary is being generated asynchronously — how is data kept from being lost?; this section keeps its original derivation and experiment, and the two accounts work at different levels rather than replacing each other.

Evidence and the limits of understanding: the candidate covers only the old prefix, so installing it must verify identity and keep the live suffix; accepted in memory and persisted successfully are different boundaries.

Verification basis:[C06] [C07] [C08] [C18] [A02] [C16]. The source is limited to commit 86e17e98c7f9 and the paths actually read; article experience, public documentation and original teaching experiments are labeled separately. See how it was obtained, what was read, and the limits.

Externally stored evidence must be locatable

"See earlier logs" is not a reliable reference. Save at least the location, version, scope and run command. If a patch is edited again after passing tests, change its status to "Old version passed, new version awaiting verification", instead of carrying over the green conclusion.

Self-check: can a large window replace long-term memory? Not completely; cross-session persistence, versions and permissions still matter. Does a summary containing every keyword preserve fidelity? Not necessarily; truth, time and certainty may have changed.

A 7.1 · Effective context engineering

Related material: Effective Context Engineering

Context engineering concerns the whole set of information the model needs this turn, beyond prompt wording. Methods include on-demand access, progressive disclosure, compaction, notes and sub-agents, but do not require every mechanism on every occasion.

Limited attention and a limited window require selecting information. More content may supply evidence but also introduce stale conclusions and noise; less content saves cost but may omit constraints.

Worked example · R07-01

More relevant information is not always better

Input and conditions. The goal is to locate an assertion failure; candidates include 20 lines of the failing function and 10000 lines of repository-wide logs.

Steps and objectsInternal actionIntermediate result / new state

2 GapExpand in a targeted way if the failure remains unexplainedInstead of loading everything at once.
3 FeedbackRecord why retrieval expanded to a particular fileThis helps judge whether retrieval has lost focus.

Join the steps up. On-demand access makes information decisions around the task; it does not mean shorter is always better.

A 7.2 · Position effects in long contexts

Related material: Lost in the Middle

Lost in the Middle studies how relevant information's position in long input affects model performance. Its conclusions come from particular models and tasks and must not be restated as inevitable failure in the middle for every new model.

Position effects are only one long-context risk. Distracting information, combining several pieces of evidence, negative conditions and version conflicts also affect engineering tasks.

A 7.3 · Context capacity and state

Related material: Claude Context Windows

Context-window documentation describes the information range a model can handle and the relevant token accounting. The window, output budget, reasoning consumption and automatic product management are different concepts; check the specific accounting against the interface.

The character count in user-visible chat cannot be treated directly as the entire input; tool definitions, results and internal state may also consume the relevant budgets.

B 7.4 · Multitask evaluation of long contexts

Related material: LongBench

LongBench evaluates long-context understanding using multiple tasks and long inputs. Tasks may require retrieval, summarization or information synthesis; one task's score cannot represent every long-task capability.

Evaluation length, language, data sources and grading all affect interpretation. Answering questions about a long article does not imply maintaining execution state across multiple rounds of code edits.

B 7.5 · Nominal window and effective capability

Related material: RULER

RULER examines the difference between nominal context length and actual effective capability by testing models across tasks and lengths. It reminds us that an interface accepting input is only the beginning of capability evaluation.

Recovering one piece of information differs from multistep reasoning that combines information; failures as length increases may also come from interference and task complexity rather than insufficient capacity alone.

A 7.6 · Opaque compaction state and continued execution

Related material: OpenAI Compaction

OpenAI Compaction provides dedicated compaction state so long interactions can continue within limited context. Some returned content is an opaque or encrypted representation; do not assume it equals an editable human-readable summary.

Applications should save and pass this state according to the official interface, without fabricating fields or claiming to recover all original content. Complete auditing still requires separately retaining original evidence.

Worked example · R07-06

A 7.8 · Proactive compaction and stage boundaries

Related material: Compaction on Demand

On-demand compaction lets context be organized proactively when needed instead of only waiting for capacity to trigger it. Task stages can be useful boundaries, such as moving from investigation to implementation, but compaction is not mandatory at every stage end.

A proactive operation adds a processing step and may affect caching and information fidelity. A task near completion may not be worth this cost.

A 7.9 · Cleaning up tool results

Related material: Context Editing

Context Editing cleans up historical content, for example old tool results. It differs from rewriting all history into a summary; the two may also affect information retention and caching differently.

B 7.10 · Threshold-triggered compaction

Related material: Compaction at a Token Threshold

Threshold-triggered compaction automatically starts organizing content based on budgets such as tokens. A threshold controls resources; it does not mean the model has understood whether the current task is ready for a handoff.

If it triggers before key evidence has been organized, a summary is more likely to lose state; if the threshold is too late, there may be too little room to complete later calls.

B 7.11 · Interaction between caching and compaction

Related material: Claude Prompt Caching

Prompt caching reuses computation for identical prefixes that meet the conditions; compaction and editing change content. They may affect one another, but the extent depends on the actual change location and product rules.

A stable prefix favors reuse, but a long irrelevant prefix may hurt context quality. A high cache-hit rate does not imply high business value.
