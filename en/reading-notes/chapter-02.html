Pretraining: how the prediction objective shapes capability

Autoregressive pretraining constructs next-token predictions from text. For target y, the loss term is−log P(y|上下文), and the optimizer updates parameters through gradients. Prediction pressure can encourage the model to learn grammar, structure and transferable patterns, but the training objective itself does not guarantee that all world knowledge is true or current.[S020][S015]

For example, increasing the true target's probability from 0.1 to 0.5 reduces negative-log loss; this does not directly imply a proportionate increase in a business task's success rate. The training distribution differs from actual tasks, and private source code or recent information may not be present in the parameters.

Data, scale and compute budget

Scaling research examines empirical relationships between loss and model size, data and computation. Chinchilla reexamines the allocation of parameters and training tokens under a fixed training budget. These provide testable patterns, not fixed ratios that apply to every architecture forever. Training-optimal, inference-optimal and business-optimal are different objectives.[S020][S021]

Data engineering covers provenance, licensing, quality filtering, deduplication, privacy, time and test contamination. The Pile demonstrates how to organize a corpus from multiple sources; deduplication research examines the effects of repeated content. A public dataset does not mean every use is unrestricted, and duplicates cannot simply be deleted on rough text similarity alone.[S022][S023]

Original examples: if training data already contains a public bug fix, success on the same issue may partly reflect memorization. Time-based splits and near-duplicate checks help identify the risk, but cannot easily prove that no similar training content existed.

SFT, RLHF and preference optimization

SFT trains task behavior with input–target-output examples. It can guide formatting, instruction following and tool calls, but incorrect demonstrations may also be learned. How non-target positions are masked depends on the training implementation.[S024]

Start with the objects and symbols used here
RLHF, the reward model and the policy
RLHF refers broadly to processes that train behavior using human feedback. A reward model scores behavior such as an answer; the policy is the probability model that actually produces actions. Their jobs differ, and a high reward is not automatically factual correctness. Independent example: if the reward model prefers long answers, the policy may learn to write at length without being more accurate; the reward criterion therefore needs to be validated.
PPO,GRPO,advantage
These are concepts from policy optimization. PPO commonly limits the update with a ratio of new to old action probabilities, plus clipping; GRPO-style methods build the advantage by comparing rewards within a group. An advantage is performance relative to a baseline, not the probability that an answer is correct. Independent raw-difference example: rewards [0,1,1] minus the group mean of 2/3 give [-2/3,1/3,1/3]. An algorithm may normalize them again, so the two forms must not be mixed.

The classic RLHF process includes supervised fine-tuning, preference data, a reward model, policy optimization and constraints relative to a reference model. Reward is a proxy objective, not objective truth; annotators may prefer fluent, confident or lengthy answers without checking facts sufficiently. PPO is a policy optimization method, not a complete RLHF system.[S024][S027]

DPO optimizes a model directly with preference pairs, avoiding the same explicit reward-model and online policy-optimization steps used in traditional RLHF. It still updates parameters and depends on data quality. Constitutional AI explores principles to guide critique, revision and AI feedback; those principles are not a formal safety proof.[S025][S026]

Original engineering analogy: if a coding agent is rewarded only for passing weak tests, it may learn to hard-code answers or modify tests. The problem may be a wrongly designed objective rather than insufficient capability. Limiting update size can improve stability but cannot correct a wrong reward.

Three kinds of "reasoning enhancement" must be distinguished

First, provide examples with intermediate reasoning in the prompt; second, improve reasoning capability through training; third, sample, score and search at runtime. These change context, parameters and control flow respectively.[S029][S030][S032][S033]

Chain-of-Thought demonstrates the value of examples with intermediate steps on its research tasks; longer does not necessarily mean more correct. Self-Consistency samples multiple paths and aggregates answers; Tree of Thoughts uses search and evaluation over candidate intermediate states. More computation may help, or may repeat a mistaken premise.

DeepSeek-R1 distinguishes R1-Zero from the R1 route that includes cold-start and other stages; it cannot be summarized as "no reasoning model needs supervised data". Verifiable math or code rewards do not automatically cover open-ended business judgment. This report should not be used to guess closed-source models' training recipes.[S030]

Process supervision and outcome supervision

Outcome supervision checks final answers; process supervision evaluates intermediate steps. The former readily obtains signals in verifiable tasks; the latter may identify processes that are correct only by chance, but has higher cost and annotation noise. Related math research does not directly imply that every software task needs step-by-step scoring.[S031]

For exclude, tests provide behavioral outcomes, while reviews of path conventions and compatibility provide process evidence. Combine them according to risk: simple changes need no repeated analysis, while cross-module logic needs fuller evidence. This is engineering advice, not a universal guarantee from a paper.

The limits of parameters, runtime state and explanations

In an ordinary session, user feedback first changes context or external memory; only an explicit training process updates model parameters. A reasoning API's internal state, visible summary and final explanation also differ. Engineering verification should require evidence and results, rather than treating long explanations as records of actual internal computation.[S037][S051]

Self-check: does longer reasoning always improve success rate? No, especially when necessary input is missing. Does a high preference score mean factual correctness? No; scoring criteria and external verification must be explicit.

A 2.1 · Scaling laws for language models

Related material: Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models

Scaling laws study empirical relationships between parameters, data, computation and language-modeling loss. They come from a particular experimental range, not natural laws that every architecture and task follows exactly; training-loss improvements cannot be converted directly into business success rates.

Larger models need enough data and optimization; under a fixed budget, each investment has an opportunity cost. The serving lifecycle also includes inference, so training-optimal does not mean optimal long-term total cost.

A 2.2 · Compute-optimal training and Chinchilla

B 2.3 · Sources and composition of training corpora

Related material: The Pile

The Pile demonstrates the organization of a multisource text dataset. A corpus is not homogeneous "internet text": documents, code, forums and papers differ in structure, quality, duplication and domain distribution, affecting the patterns the model learns.

Data governance needs provenance, permissions and licensing, privacy, format cleaning and training–test separation. Public accessibility does not imply unrestricted use. This book discusses engineering checks and offers no unverified legal judgments.

B 2.4 · Training-data deduplication

Related material: Deduplicating Training Data Makes Language Models Better

Training-data deduplication handles exact and near-duplicate content; research examines its effects on memorization, generalization and evaluation overlap. Exact and approximate deduplication differ in granularity, compute cost and the risk of incorrect deletion.

More aggressive deduplication is not always better. Similar code templates may have different semantics, and document-version differences may record important changes. Decide whether to work at document, paragraph, function or chunk level, and retain necessary provenance.

C 2.5 · Data, scaling and training evaluation

Related material: CS336 Data Scaling Evaluation

When returning to CS336's data, scaling and evaluation material, shift the focus to the training system: batch construction, objective alignment, optimizer, numeric stability and experiment records together determine whether results can be interpreted.

A 2.7 · Direct preference optimization: DPO

Related material: Direct Preference Optimization

DPO optimizes a model directly with preference pairs in relation to a reference policy, avoiding the same explicit reward-model and online policy-optimization steps used in typical RLHF. It still needs data, parameter training and evaluation; it is not a prompt technique requiring no training.

If preference pairs reward only length or confidence, the model may learn incorrect behavior. Reference constraints do not automatically correct label bias; training data and objective definitions remain central.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
sigmoid: maps a single number into the range between 0 and 1
The function rule is sigmoid(x)=1/(1+exp(-x)). It computes number by number and does not require one row of results to sum to 1, so it cannot stand in directly for a softmax over several candidates. Independent micro-example: sigmoid(0)=0.5; computing two zeros separately gives [0.5,0.5], which is only a coincidence — for the input [1,1] the two terms already sum to more than 1.
π, π_ref and β: the symbols of a preference objective
In policy and preference optimization, π denotes a conditional-probability policy and π_ref a fixed reference policy; β is a coefficient that sets the scale of the objective or the strength of the constraint. The probability of a complete answer is built from the conditional probability of each token.π here is not the circle constant, and β is not the LayerNorm offset parameter. The w/l in the DPO expression mark the preferred and the non-preferred answer, not a weight w and a layer count L.

B 2.9 · Proximal policy optimization: PPO

B 2.13 · Process supervision and outcome supervision

B 2.14 · Multipath sampling and self-consistency

Related material: Self-Consistency

Self-Consistency samples multiple reasoning paths and aggregates their final answers. It adds inference-time computation, rather than cross-checking multiple independent factual sources.
