Evaluation defines success rather than grading attractive answers

Agent evaluation includes tasks, trials, graders, traces and actual environmental outcomes. The final answer is only part of the evidence. Code success must meet the goal without breaking regressions; retrieval success must find necessary evidence; recovery success must also avoid duplicated side effects. Percentages with different definitions cannot be compared directly.[S143][S144][S145]

Original samples should preserve goals, baselines, authorization, constraints, acceptance criteria, budgets and fault types. Do not make one fixed tool path the only correct process unless the path itself is a task requirement.

Outcomes, processes and model judges

Deterministic grading suits tests, files and database state; model judges can assess semantics or subjective criteria, but are affected by position, verbosity and preferences. A high score is not factual proof; human calibration and independent evidence are needed.[S148]

Original combination: tests verify behavior, static checks enforce boundaries, spot checks assess maintainability, and traces explain failures. Do not average serious security failures away in an overall score or reward mechanically completing a long process.

pass@1,pass@k,pass^k

Start with the objects and symbols used here
pass@k and pass^k
pass@k-style metrics usually care about at least one success in k attempts; pass^k-style metrics stress succeeding every time. The exact basis of an estimate depends on the benchmark, and stopping after one success must not be conflated with being reliable every time. Under an independent, equal-probability assumption of p=0.8, at least one success in 3 attempts is 0.992 and all 3 succeeding is 0.512. If real tasks are correlated or differ in difficulty, it has to be computed differently.

pass@1 concerns one normal attempt, pass@k concerns at least one success when k attempts are allowed, and pass^k emphasizes reliability across repeated successes. τ-bench examines stability through repeated execution.[S153][S143]

Start with the objects and symbols used here
C(n,k), !: the number of combinations and the factorial
n! means the integers from 1 to n multiplied together, with 0!=1; C(n,k)=n!/(k!(n-k)!) is the number of ways to choose k of n distinct objects without replacement and without regard to order. It is not a model confidence C. Independent example: C(4,2)=6. With n=10, c=3 and k=2, 1−C(7,2)/C(10,2)=24/45, about 0.533333.

Original idealized example: with independent attempts and p=0.8, the probability of at least one success in three attempts is 1-(1-p)^3=0.992, while success on all three is p^3=0.512. Real attempts may be correlated, so this cannot be applied unconditionally. The common estimator 1-C(n-c,k)/C(n,k) also requires a matching sampling definition and k≤n.

The best single demonstration therefore cannot represent the normal single-attempt experience. Also state whether selection, retries, tool verification and extra budgets are allowed.

Engineering mechanisms and failure boundaries

Locating a regression from the four-cell table

Both old and new correct on 12, fixed 5, regressed 1, both wrong on 2; compute 13/20 for the old and 17/20 for the new, then explain each of the 6 disagreeing tasks.

Key boundary: repeating the same question is not an independent task; sampling and changes of environment have to be recorded.

Follow it further through the source or the engineering basis: keep the task, the trial, the grading and the statistical unit consistent. Do not check only the path that goes well; check the behavior on cancellation, on a version change, and when the result is unknown.

For the full mechanism and a continuous example see Why can pass@k, pass^k and a single-attempt success rate not be used interchangeably?; this section keeps its own derivation and experiment, and the two accounts work at different levels rather than replacing each other.

Evidence and the limits of understanding: record the task, a single attempt, the execution trace and the environment's result separately; resource limits and state that was not reset both confound a comparison.

Verification basis:[A07] [A12]. The source is limited to commit 86e17e98c7f9 and the paths actually read; article experience, public documentation and original teaching experiments are labeled separately. See how it was obtained, what was read, and the limits.

Controls and ablations

An original experiment fixes code and environment, gives old and new approaches the same tasks, and groups them as simple, cross-module, long-context and restricted-permission tasks. Changing one mechanism at a time aids attribution; changing the model, prompt and retrieval together evaluates only their combination.

Report uncertainty with small samples. Bootstrap by task can estimate an interval for the difference; repeated runs of the same task are correlated and cannot all be treated as independent samples. The experiment appendix uses synthetic data for demonstration, not measured product rankings.

The limits of benchmarks

SWE-bench tests real issue fixes, with the specific subset to be stated; LiveCodeBench focuses on continuing updates and contamination; AgentBench and GAIA cover different environments and tasks. Completion, algorithmic code, tool-based business tasks and repository fixes are different capabilities.[S149][S150][S152][S154][S155]

Research on infrastructure noise shows that CPU, memory, timeouts and execution limits affect scores. Comparisons must control the environment, concurrency, networking and retries, rather than attributing every infrastructure difference to the model.[S151]

Failure categories point to the layer to fix

This book suggests classifying by the first critical break: wrong goal, missing evidence, misread evidence, tool choice, argument permissions, environmental failure, implementation errors, insufficient verification and inconsistent recovery. Classification locates issues; it does not add a new approval system.

Costs should include failed attempts and necessary rework. Counting only the tokens in the successful attempt underestimates true cost. Keep quality, efficiency, reliability and security separate where possible, so a single score does not hide important regressions.

Evaluation assets must survive platform changes

On the verification date 2026-10-17, documentation for OpenAI's old Evals platform listed plans for read-only access on 2026-10-31 and closure on 2026-11-30; these must not be described as already having occurred. Task data, grading logic and traces should be portable, and methods should not depend on the old interface.[S146][S147]

Self-check: does higher pass@k mean a better single attempt? Not necessarily. What if all tests pass but complexity rises? Record behavior and maintainability separately, and judge whether the complexity is necessary.

A 12.1 · A complete method for agent evaluation

Related material: Demystifying Evals for AI Agents

Agent evaluation includes tasks, trials, graders and execution traces. Observe final answers, actual environmental outcomes and costs separately; do not rely only on the model's own completion claim.

Starting evaluation from real failures can provide value quickly, but samples should gradually cover neighboring scenarios rather than optimizing only known issues.

A 12.3 · Execution trace grading

Related material: Trace Grading

Trace Grading assesses decisions, tools and evidence use during execution. It helps locate errors, but should not reward mechanical imitation of one fixed example path.

Different search orders can be equally correct. Only evidence or actions actually required by the task should become necessary conditions.

B 12.5 · Datasets and grading criteria

Related material: Getting Started with Datasets

Dataset construction should represent the real task distribution and distinguish training or tuning uses from independent evaluation. Repeatedly changing a system based on the same test set gradually overfits it.

Samples should include successes and failures, simple and complex cases, boundaries and exceptions. Collecting only examples where the new approach excels exaggerates its gains.

B 12.6 · Model judges and grading bias

Related material: Judging LLM-as-a-Judge

LLM-as-a-Judge studies the capabilities and biases of model judges, including position, style and length effects. Model evaluation can scale up, but is not inherently objective truth.

A judge needs clear criteria, calibration on independent samples and necessary human spot checks. Incorrect knowledge shared with the evaluated model may produce shared bias.

B 12.11 · Rules, tools and repeated success rates

Related material: tau-bench

τ-bench evaluates tools, user interaction and rule following, using repeated runs to examine reliability. One success and success every time are different goals.

Under ideal conditions of independence and a fixed success rate, the probability of at least one success is 1-(1-p)^k, while success on every attempt is p^k; real attempts are often correlated, so these formulas cannot be applied unconditionally.
