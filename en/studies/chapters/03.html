1. The data pipeline is part of model behavior

Pretraining does not feed raw web pages straight into a GPU. It requires decisions about sampling scope, cleaning and deduplication, encoding, sequence packing, length distribution, training/validation splits and traceable data versions. Every step changes the distribution the model actually sees. Excessively removing short lines may damage code; deduplicating by page URL may retain many mirrors; random paragraph-level training/test splits may put versions of the same document on both sides.

A minimal data manifest should answer: Which snapshot did each sample come from? Which filters were applied? Which Tokenizer encoded it? How are valid tokens counted? Was the validation set isolated before deduplication? Proving that a model can solve a problem requires ruling out the explanation that its answer or a highly similar solution leaked into training/retrieval data; final accuracy alone is insufficient.

When business data is used to fine-tune a model, permission and deletion responsibilities extend from the original data to training artifacts. Deleting a database record alone does not mean model parameters have forgotten it. This is also why sensitive, frequently changing business facts often belong in an external retrieval layer with access controls rather than entering the training set blindly.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
Optimizers, Adam and AdamW
An optimizer turns gradients into parameter updates. Adam also keeps, for every parameter, a moving average m of the gradient and a moving average v of the squared gradient; these are training state, not attention values. AdamW decouples weight decay from the adaptive gradient update. Independent first-step example: with g=2, old m=0 and β₁=0.9, the new m is 0.2, and after bias correction m_hat=2. The 0.2 cannot be taken as the full size of the update.

AdamW maintains the first and second moments of gradients and uses them after bias correction to normalize step sizes. Decoupled weight decay applies shrinkage directly in parameter updates; it should not simply be equated with applying L2 penalties uniformly to all parameters. Implementations specify which parameters decay, when accumulation occurs and how the learning rate is scheduled. Reproducing an experiment requires saving these configurations, not only a learning-rate number.

Gradient accumulation combines contributions from several microbatches into one optimizer update. Equivalence depends on loss normalization, stochastic operators and how schedule steps are counted. With different numbers of valid tokens in long and short sequences, a simple mean of microbatch means may differ from the mean over all valid tokens. State whether the objective weights samples or tokens equally before designing accumulation logic.

AdamW's "historical state" is accounted for separately from this step's gradient

How to read the changes in the figure

Do not write the parameter update as 0.1×0.2 just because m is 0.2. The bias correction and the normalization by the squared scale have not been done yet.

m and v are optimizer state, not forward activations of the network. Resuming training with only w loaded, and not these states, changes the trajectory that follows.

If the loss has been scaled up under mixed precision, the scale must be undone before the gradients are clipped; otherwise what is clipped is the scaled-up number, not the gradient that was meant to be limited.

Do not conflate: the figure above is the first step, with ε left out; not every step reduces to g/|g|. Later on, m and v carry history with them.

3. Why VRAM is not just parameter count times 2 bytes

Start with the objects and symbols used here
Mixed precision, loss scaling and master weights
Mixed precision uses different numeric formats for different computations or states. A master copy of the weights can be kept at higher precision as the baseline for updates; loss scaling enlarges the loss before the backward pass and then scales the gradients back down, lowering the risk of underflow at some low precisions. This is not multiplying the learning rate up at will. Whether master weights or loss scaling are actually needed depends on the precision format, the optimizer and the implementation.

Training may simultaneously hold model weights, gradients, optimizer moments, master-weight copies, intermediate activations, temporary operator buffers and communication buffers. Some common mixed-precision Adam configurations approach 16 bytes of model state per parameter, but this is not a universal constant: FP32 master weights, gradient dtype, sharding and quantized optimizers all change it.

Write the estimate by component: M_total = M_weights + M_grads + M_optimizer + M_activations + M_workspace + M_communication. Judging trainability only from "7B×2 bytes=14GB" is wrong; at most, this estimates weight storage for a particular dtype. Longer sequences also increase activation costs even if model parameters stay unchanged.

Start with the objects and symbols used here
checkpoint: you must look at the object that was saved
A model checkpoint saves weights or training state; activation checkpointing saves a few intermediate nodes so they can be recomputed on the backward pass; an agent task checkpoint saves how far execution has got. The three share a name, but what they restore is entirely different. When you see "checkpoint", first ask whether it restores parameters, recomputes activations, or continues a task. An agent saving a summary does not update model weights.

Activation checkpointing saves VRAM by not storing some intermediate values and recomputing them during backpropagation. This checkpoint is an autodiff memory optimization, not an agent task checkpoint or persistent training state. The same English word has different meanings at three system layers; make the distinction explicit.

How to read the changes in the figure

Counting only weight storage, inference comes to 2000B, while the persistent state for training in this example is already 16000B. Activations, communication buffers and allocation fragmentation are not yet included.

Gradient accumulation splits a large batch into several small ones to run, which mainly controls peak activation; it does not make all the parameters disappear.

Activation checkpointing stores fewer intermediate values but requires recomputation; ZeRO-style sharding spreads the different states across devices but introduces communication. Which account is being saved on has to be stated clearly.

Do not conflate: the exact byte count varies with the dtype, the master copy, the optimizer implementation and the sharding strategy; 16B is not a fixed constant for every training job.

4. How distributed training divides work

Start with the objects and symbols used here
MoE, experts and the router
MoE is a model structure that contains several sub-networks; the router selects or weights a few experts for the current token's representation. An expert is usually a numeric sub-network, not a chat agent given a prompt such as "math teacher". Independent example: with 4 experts and 2 chosen per token, one pass activates only 2 of the expert paths; the parameters and routing state that the model design requires still have to be stored.
DP, TP, PP, ZeRO and FSDP
Data parallelism lets several copies of the model process different samples and synchronize their gradients; tensor parallelism splits the parameters and the computation of a single operator; pipeline parallelism puts different layers on different devices. ZeRO/FSDP-style techniques also shard parameters, gradients or optimizer state. What they split differs: dividing one layer's matrix in two is not the same as giving two independent user tasks to two agents.

Data parallelism lets devices process different samples, then synchronize gradients; tensor parallelism splits a layer's matrix computations and requires communication within that layer; pipeline parallelism splits layers, with microbatches flowing across stages and creating pipeline bubbles; expert parallelism assigns MoE experts to devices, with routing moving tokens between devices. They address different constraints and cannot be summarized as "More GPUs make training linearly faster".

Before choosing parallelism, determine whether the bottleneck is fitting on one GPU, matrix computation or the inter-device network. Splitting a large matrix too finely may increase the communication share; uneven pipeline stages let the slowest stage limit the whole; ignoring expert load distribution leaves popular experts queuing. Lesson 59 continues these trade-offs through roofline and bandwidth.

Sharding optimizer state, gradients and parameters reduces what one device holds but introduces collection and communication. Checkpoint recovery also needs optimizer state, random state, data position and schedule counters; otherwise resuming from the same weights need not reproduce the same training trajectory. Production recovery seeks explicit recovery semantics, without requiring bitwise identity on every kind of hardware.

Engineering case · D02

Reading a real fix: from the gradient-accumulation incident to a test that tells the wrong objective apart

Where the original engineering evidence is

Transformers PR #34191, titled "Fix Gradient Accumulation issue", was merged on 2024-10-17. This section pins loss_utils.py from the merge commit: when num_items_in_batch is present, fixed_cross_entropy selects sum reduction, then divides by the number supplied by the caller; ForCausalLMLoss first shifts predictions and labels, then flattens them.[HFPR] This makes responsibility for normalization by the total valid-item count traceable across code boundaries, beyond a blog's conclusion.[GRAD]

Parameter names cannot replace counting definitions

Check the caller to determine whether num_items_in_batch means samples, labels or valid tokens across an accumulation group; this small function cannot prove the caller's denominator correct. In particular, boundary changes after shifting, ignore_index, differing rank counts and entirely empty batches may make the count differ from the items actually entering cross entropy.

This book performed two levels of verification

The first is a small squared-error example with gradients of −1 and 0, easy to calculate by hand. The second actually uses torch.cross_entropy: the gradient from one forward pass over the same 4 samples matches accumulation over groups of 1/3 items normalized by the same total; simply averaging each batch's mean differs. The original gradient matrices are available in the results below.

How to apply this to your Trainer

First test large-batch/accumulation equivalence on one device with fixed small data, no dropout and no random augmentation. Then enable AMP, DDP, clipping and the actual data loader, adding one kind of factor at a time. If the framework already handles the global denominator, do not multiply by world_size again. For objectives without equal token weights, such as equal weight per conversation, write the intended objective separately instead of copying this formula.

A follow-up by counterexample

"The loss falls, so everything is fine" does not hold: a wrong objective can fall too. "The gradients are roughly close" is insufficient as well: first ask whether the objective definition explains the difference, then discuss floating-point error. The historical PR supports the problem mechanism; it is not evidence that the current version still has the bug.

How to read the changes in the figure

DP does not necessarily make the same step of a single request faster; what it adds more naturally is capacity for independent samples. It is TP that splits one operator, at the cost of extra communication.

PP puts different layers on different devices, which shares out the capacity; a single micro-batch still has to pass through the earlier layers before the later ones, and only with several micro-batches is there pipeline overlap.

One system can combine these strategies, but every further layer of partitioning means tracking data movement and synchronization. With the matrix sizes but no network bandwidth, the net gain is hard to judge.

Do not conflate: "two cards" does not give "twice the speed"; if the added communication outweighs the computation saved, it can even be slower.

5. How to use scaling laws

Training loss has empirical relationships with parameter count, data volume and compute that can be fitted, but they depend on data, architecture, optimization and budget ranges. Chinchilla discusses allocating parameters and data under fixed training compute; ratios from its particular research setting are not eternal optima for every model, language or inference cost.[N37]

Also distinguish compute-optimal training from optimal total deployment cost. If a model will be called many times, training a smaller model on more tokens may reduce long-term inference costs. Conversely, a model project used for one exploration may not justify substantial extra training to save inference costs. The actual objective includes training, serving and quality gains, beyond one loss curve.

Reports must separate training loss, validation loss and business success rate. A small gain in validation cross entropy does not guarantee better judgments of tool-call permissions; a higher code pass rate may be concentrated in certain languages. Expanded capabilities need stratified evaluation, not inference from parameter scale.

6. How to read this book's actual training experiment

train_tiny_lm.pycontains two causal Transformer layers and 18,320 parameters, trained on a CPU for 100 steps. The data is an author-constructed cyclic sequence of 16 symbols; the task is to learn "The next symbol is the current symbol plus one, modulo 16". This makes gradients, masks and updates verifiable without passing the experiment off as a general language model.

In the accompanying run record, training cross entropy falls from about 2.8468 to 0.00345, and training-token accuracy reaches 1.0. This proves the code can optimize its defined objective and memorize/fit this simple rule; it does not prove test generalization, multistep reasoning or actual coding capability. Changing future inputs leaves zero error in prefix logits, separately verifying the causal mask.

Further experiments should do more than add training steps: reverse validation sequences, change symbol mappings, introduce different rules and compare in-distribution with out-of-distribution results. Check whether failures come from model capacity, data coverage or an unidentifiable task. Retain training seeds and configuration and repeat at least a few seeds to judge whether a gain is just an initialization coincidence.

7. The conclusion standard for a senior engineer

When a report says "More data, stronger model", ask about valid training tokens, deduplication and contamination, compute budgets, deployment costs and task scope. For "Out of VRAM", break down usage rather than immediately reducing the batch. For "Loss is not decreasing", check labels, masks, gradients, learning rate and numerics before changing architecture. Every check must correspond to an observable variable; that is understanding the training system from within.

BatchNorm: statistics across samples, unlike LayerNorm
BatchNorm usually computes its statistics for one feature or channel along the batch axis and the applicable spatial axes; LayerNorm computes along the feature dimensions designated inside one sample. BatchNorm may use statistics from different sources in training and in evaluation, and the details still depend on the configuration. The word Norm alone is no reason to mix up the statistics axes of different normalization layers; nor should two independent users come to share one session context because of it.

Why accumulating gradients over four microbatches does not necessarily equal four times the learning rate

Input and conditions. The effective batch has 4 equally sized microbatches, with loss averaged by sample; their mean gradients for one parameter are 1, 2, 3 and 4.

Steps and objectsInternal actionIntermediate result / new state
1 Target gradientThe mean over the full effective batch(1+2+3+4)/4=2.5.
2 Incorrect direct accumulationBackpropagate each mean loss without dividing by 4The gradient sum is 10, amplified 4 times.
3 Correct scalingDivide each microbatch loss by 4 firstAccumulates to 2.5.
4 Update timingFour backward calls, then one stepNot an update after every microbatch treated afterward as one large batch.
5 Limits of equivalenceWith cross-sample BatchNorm, random layers or different optimizer step countsNumerics and dynamics may differ; full equivalence cannot be claimed unconditionally.

Join the steps up. Gradient accumulation requires explicit loss-reduction denominators, microbatch sizes and optimizer update counts.

Limits and counterexamples. Unequal microbatches require weighting by sample or valid-token counts, not mechanically dividing by the number of microbatches.
