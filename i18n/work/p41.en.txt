6032	1. Compaction is a lossy information transformation
14488	Original history H contains goals, tool feedback, attempts, assumptions and verified facts. A compaction function produces C(H), from which the model continues acting. The question is whether C(H) increases decision errors over future task distribution Q, not its literal similarity to the original. A short summary may preserve every currently important condition; a long one may lose the sole crucial negation.
6996	Anthropic discusses compaction, notes and sub-agents in context engineering; OpenAI also documents opaque compaction state. These show forms beyond ordinary summaries, but form alone cannot establish lossless downstream tasks.
20865	This book adopts an engineering objective: prioritize constraints, conclusion status, evidence entry points and unfinished work that affect later actions. This is not a universally optimal compaction algorithm, but a design testable through task recovery experiments.
6285	2. The greatest danger is not losing a few background details
14695	Original: "Suspect the Skill was not loaded, but logs are unchecked, so this is unconfirmed." Bad summary: "The unloaded Skill caused the failure." A hypothesis becomes fact, directing later edits toward a wrong root cause.
14694	Original: "Change only description, without changing skill content or adding a router." Bad summary: "Optimize skill routing." Negative constraints disappear and scope may expand.
14696	Original: "Tests passed on commit A; file B changed afterward." Bad summary: "All tests passed." Lost evidence freshness creates false completion.

6043	1. Retrieval loses information stage by stage
22958	A user question becomes a query; retrievers obtain corpus candidates; permission and version filters constrain visibility; ranking selects limited results; the context compiler trims again; finally the model uses evidence. If key material disappears at any stage, even a strong downstream model may answer wrongly.
18525	RAG therefore needs more than a "vector database → LLM" diagram. Know chunking, index creation time, query encoding, result completeness and how evidence supports answers. Contextual Retrieval adds chunk context and combines retrieval signals, but gains must be remeasured on your own tasks.

6263	2. BM25: term-frequency saturation and length normalization
20866	This book uses a common nonnegative IDF form:
25761	Rarer terms usually have larger idf; repeated occurrences within one document give saturating gains, not linear rewards for a hundred repetitions. Length normalization reduces advantages from mere verbosity. Implementations differ in IDF and query-frequency handling; fix the specific formula for comparisons.
11940	The code splits snake_case and camelCase, then matches English alphanumeric words and individual Chinese characters. 6408	3. Dense Retrieval and vector spaces
14791	Two-tower retrieval usually encodes queries and documents separately and scores relevance by dot product or cosine. Documents can be encoded offline and queries online; training positives, negatives and task instructions shape the similarity it supports. Similar descriptions need not mean identical facts; semantic approximation may hide opposite conditions, versions and exact identifiers.
16324	Cosine commonly uses normalized dot products; if an inner-product index receives unexpectedly unnormalized vectors, norms affect ranking. Changing embedding models, dimensions, preprocessing or instruction templates usually requires rebuilding related indexes; do not mix different vector spaces in one collection.
7313	A Cross-encoder jointly encodes queries and documents for finer relevance judgments, but candidate-pair counts directly increase cost. It suits reranking small retrieved sets and cannot recover missing candidates. ColBERT retains multi-token representations with late interaction between single-vector efficiency and finer matching, with more complex storage and indexes.
6517	4. Why ANN is faster and why it may miss results
24204	Exact vector search scans every vector, giving a straightforward correctness baseline. Approximate nearest-neighbor indexes trade controlled recall loss for speed or storage. HNSW navigates candidate neighbors through layered graphs; more search candidates usually improve recall at greater cost. IVF selects coarse-cluster buckets, then searches within them; too few probes miss results, too many approach scanning. PQ encodes vector blocks as codebook indexes, reducing storage while approximating distances.
11157	Do not conflate ANN Recall@k with business-evidence recall. ANN metrics may only compare with exact vector rankings and show few neighbors lost; if the embedding never ranked the right document highly, even strong ANN recall cannot guarantee business success.
12642	Metadata filters affect ANN behavior. Global top-k followed by permission filtering may leave only one or two items, withholding findable material from authorized users. Options include prefiltering, appropriate over-retrieval, partitioning or filter-aware indexes; measure recall and latency under strict ACLs and highly selective filters.
