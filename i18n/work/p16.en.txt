27816	Follow-up: when is parallel research by several agents worth it?
16388	When the sub-questions can be searched relatively independently and the cost of integration is less than the gain from parallelism. For instance, one looks into the model mechanism and another into engineering failures, and at the end they are merged along the shared structure of the question. If every agent searches the same material over again, the extra calls only magnify the redundancy.
28063	Follow-up: when does the research end?
18203	It ends when the key claims have enough support, the main counterexamples have been checked, the remaining uncertainty does not change the current decision, or the budget reaches its limit. 12748	Start with a ledger of claims
19991	The teaching question "is the new routing worth shipping" splits into at least six kinds of claim: recall of the skills actually needed, wrong loading, task success, time taken, cost and regressions. 23367	Which uncertainty each round of research should reduce
24057	The first round locates the tasks that really fail; the second follows the trajectory to see whether the rule was loaded; the third constructs a fix that holds the other conditions constant; the fourth runs the paired tasks and the counterexamples. 16385	How the sub-studies are merged

23327	How a knowledge graph serves the main text

26749	Limits and counterexamples.</strong> The scalar worked example only refutes the simple inference that "a high weight must mean a high contribution"; real causal analysis has to control for distribution shift and for other paths.
28043	Follow-up: is the token with the highest attention weight the reason for the answer?
11115	That cannot be inferred directly. The output also passes through the
19293	projection, several heads, residuals, the
15611	and later layers; a high weight is not the only contribution, and low-weight information may also affect the output by another path. A more systematic attribution or intervention experiment is needed.
27961	Follow-up: once a feature for dangerous behavior is found, can the risk be removed once and for all?
11015	There is no guarantee. The behavior may be spread across several features and paths, and an intervention may also damage normal capability. Evaluation across several situations and a defensive layer are needed; one internal feature must not be treated as a complete safety switch.
27774	Follow-up: why is interpretability still worth learning?
16492	It helps tell "a behavior was observed" apart from "the mechanism that produced it is understood", and it provides methods for studying a model's representations and causal effects. For a user, the most important gain is recognizing how strong or weak a piece of explanatory evidence is, rather than memorizing a batch of feature names.
17876	Engineering deep dive · E54 · continues Q108
25869	The share read and the final contribution are different objects
19946	In a one-dimensional teaching aggregation with weights [0.9,0.1] and Value [0,100], the contributions are [0,10]. The first position, which has the largest attention, contributes 0, while the second supplies the entire aggregated value. A more complex network also has multi-head projections, residuals, nonlinearities and later layers. Looking at the size of the weights alone therefore cannot explain the final output directly.
20863	This small counterexample in the book proves only that "the largest weight does not entail the largest numeric contribution"; it does not claim to have carried out causal attribution on a real model. Real interpretability research has to trace internal paths, construct interventions and acknowledge the scope within which its results apply.
23180	Where a correlational visualization and a causal experiment differ
15251	A visualization shows a correlated quantity from the run at hand; a causal question asks "what happens if this is changed while the other conditions are held constant". Masking a token, replacing an activation or switching off a head can each affect several paths, and can even push the model outside its training distribution. Observing a change does not by itself prove that this is the only cause during a natural run.
20476	A more reliable experiment uses controlled inputs, an explicit object of intervention and comparable output metrics, and checks for alternative paths and side effects. Ablating an attention head and changing the input text are not equivalent interventions, and not every change can be attributed to one highlighted block.
24932	Self-reported reasoning cannot replace internal evidence either
21664	A model can give a coherent explanation, but whether that text is faithful to the real computation needs separate verification. Finding some of the paths in research is not the same as being able to read the model's thought in full. Do not treat the "thinking process" a user sees as an engineering log or as proof of execution.
28108	Follow-up: so is a heat map of no use at all?
20600	It is. It can help find a mask bug, an abnormal concentration or a position offset, and can guide a research hypothesis. But its use should be written up as "diagnosing an intermediate computation or generating a hypothesis", not as "proving why the model answered this way". The more intuitive a figure, the more its subject needs stating.
27906	Follow-up: how is a teaching animation kept from misleading?
14043	In the animation, label the input, the weights, the Value, the output and the simplifying conditions in step; recompute with pinned parameters; say which layers were left out. Keep real model data separate from a hand-made sketch. A handsome figure should not draw a mechanism that is not yet known as a definite causal chain.
7586	E54: how to read this result, rather than only looking at "pass"
22357	The item with the largest attention share may have a Value of zero; a smaller share times a very large Value can still dominate the output. The brightness of a heat map is not a complete causal explanation.
24885	Self-check:</strong> with attention [0.9,0.1] and Value [0,100], does the first position contribute more?
10854	No. The item-by-item contributions are 0 and 10. An attention share has to be combined with the content and with the later transforms; the cause of an answer cannot be inferred from heat-map brightness alone.
